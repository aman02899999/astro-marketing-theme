Email is still the highest-ROI marketing channel — $36 back for every $1 spent, according to industry benchmarks. But most email programs underperform because the writing is generic, the sequences are too short, and the CTAs are weak.

These prompts fix all three problems.

## Subject Line Prompts

### Prompt 1: Subject Line Generator

```
Write 20 email subject lines for:

Email topic: [what the email is about]
Audience: [who will receive it]
Goal: [open rate / click / conversion]
Brand tone: [casual / professional / witty / urgent]

Generate 4 subject lines in each style:
- Curiosity gap ("The one thing most [audience] get wrong about [topic]")
- Direct benefit ("How to [outcome] in [timeframe]")
- Urgency/scarcity ("[X hours left / spots remaining]")
- Question format ("Are you making this [topic] mistake?")
- Personalization hook ("Why [common belief] is costing you [outcome]")

Flag the top 3 you recommend testing first and explain why.
```

---

### Prompt 2: Subject Line Split Test Pairs

```
Create 5 A/B test pairs for email subject lines about [topic].

For each pair:
- Version A: [your hypothesis — e.g. curiosity-based]
- Version B: [alternative — e.g. direct benefit]

Include a hypothesis for what you expect to win and why.
Audience: [your list description]
```

## Welcome Sequence Prompts

### Prompt 3: SaaS Welcome Onboarding Sequence

```
Write a 7-email onboarding sequence for a SaaS product:

Product: [name and what it does]
New user goal: [what success looks like in week 1]
Key features to drive adoption: [list 3–4]
Common drop-off point: [where users typically churn]

Email 1 (Hour 1): Welcome + first action to take (should take under 5 minutes)
Email 2 (Day 1): Key feature tutorial + social proof
Email 3 (Day 3): Use case inspiration / customer story
Email 4 (Day 5): Feature they probably haven't discovered
Email 5 (Day 7): 7-day check-in — offer help
Email 6 (Day 14): Power user tip
Email 7 (Day 30): Success milestone email + upsell/referral ask

Each email: subject line, body (100–200 words), one clear CTA.
```

---

### Prompt 4: E-commerce Post-Purchase Sequence

```
Write a post-purchase email sequence for an e-commerce brand:

Product purchased: [product type]
Brand personality: [adjectives describing your brand]
Customer profile: [who bought this]

Email 1 (Immediate): Order confirmation + what to expect
Email 2 (Day 2): Shipping update + usage tips or excitement builder
Email 3 (Day 7): Product arrived — check-in + how to get the most from it
Email 4 (Day 14): Review request + referral incentive
Email 5 (Day 30): Related product recommendation based on purchase

Each email: subject, preview text, body, CTA.
```

## Newsletter Prompts

### Prompt 5: Weekly Newsletter Template

```
Create a reusable weekly newsletter template for:

Brand: [company name]
Newsletter name: [if you have one]
Audience: [subscribers and what they care about]
Core value: [what every issue must deliver]
Sections to include: [e.g. industry news / tip of the week / tool spotlight / CTA]

Produce:
1. Newsletter structure template with section names and word counts
2. Subject line formula to reuse each week
3. Opening line formula (hook that works every week)
4. Sign-off style that builds relationship
5. Example full issue using this template for the topic: [your sample topic]
```

---

### Prompt 6: Re-engagement Campaign

```
Write a 3-email re-engagement campaign for inactive subscribers:

Definition of inactive: [haven't opened in X months]
List size: [approx number of inactive subscribers]
Brand: [description]
Incentive available: [discount / free resource / exclusive content — or none]

Email 1: "We miss you" — light and curiosity-driven
Email 2: Value reminder — best content or offer they may have missed
Email 3: Final ask — explicit unsubscribe option if not interested

Goal: Re-activate engaged subscribers, remove the truly uninterested.
Each email: subject, body, CTA.
```

## Automation and Segmentation Prompts

### Prompt 7: Behavioral Trigger Email

```
Write a behavioral trigger email for:

Trigger event: [e.g. "user viewed pricing page 3 times without converting"]
User profile: [what you know about them]
Goal: [move them to the next step]

The email should:
- Reference the behavior without being creepy about it
- Address the most likely reason they haven't converted
- Offer a low-friction next step
- Include a secondary CTA if the primary doesn't resonate

Subject line: [3 options]
Body: under 150 words
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long should marketing emails be?

It depends entirely on the goal. Cold outreach: under 100 words. Newsletters: 300–800 words. Sales sequences: 150–250 words per email. Promotional emails: 100–200 words. The rule: every word must earn its place. ChatGPT tends to write long — always ask it to trim by 30%.

### What's the best email open rate to aim for?

Industry average is 20–35% depending on your sector. If your list is warm and opted-in, target 35–50%. Subject line is the biggest lever. Run A/B tests with Prompt 2 consistently and your open rates will compound over time.

### How do I avoid the spam folder?

Technical: authenticate your domain (SPF, DKIM, DMARC). Content: avoid spam trigger words (free, guaranteed, urgent, click here), keep image-to-text ratio balanced, and maintain a clean list. Behavioral: high open and click rates signal to ISPs that your emails are wanted.

### Should I use AI for every email or just some?

Use AI for first drafts, sequences, variations, and when you're stuck. Don't use it when the email needs to come from a real person with real context — customer service replies, personal founder emails, and account manager outreach work best when genuinely written by a human.

### How often should I email my list?

Minimum once a week to stay top of mind. Maximum is what your content quality can sustain. Daily is viable if every email delivers genuine value. The unsubscribe rate tells you everything — if it spikes, you're over-mailing or under-delivering value.
