LinkedIn is the only platform where professional content compounds indefinitely. A post from 2 years ago still drives connections today. These prompts help you build a content strategy that grows your professional reputation and generates real business opportunities.

## Content Strategy Prompts

### Prompt 1: LinkedIn Content Pillars

```
Design a LinkedIn content strategy for:

Your role: [job title and industry]
Your goal: [job opportunities / client leads / speaking gigs / industry authority / team building]
Your unique expertise: [what you know that others in your field don't]
Your audience: [who follows you — or who you want following you]
Time available: [X posts per week]

Strategy:
1. Positioning statement (your LinkedIn "lane" — what you're known for)
2. Content pillars (3–4 recurring themes that reinforce your positioning)
3. Content types to rotate: [stories / lessons learned / takes / how-tos / curated / personal]
4. Hook formula for your niche (what makes your audience stop scrolling)
5. First 8 post ideas (2 per pillar)
6. Your "signature post format" (the style your best content will use)
7. Engagement tactics (how to grow through comments, not just posts)
```

### Prompt 2: LinkedIn Post Writer

```
Write a LinkedIn post about: [topic]

My expertise: [your background]
Key insight: [what you want to share — your specific take]
Audience: [who follows you]
Goal: [engagement / drive to profile / generate leads / share knowledge]

LinkedIn post structure:
LINE 1 (hook): [statement or question that stops the scroll — under 12 words, creates a pattern interrupt]
LINE 2: blank line
LINES 3–8: [the substance — story, lesson, list, or insight — short paragraphs, one idea each]
LINE 9: blank line
LINE 10–11: [the key takeaway or "so what"]
LINE 12: blank line
LINE 13 (CTA): [follow / comment with / save / DM me — pick one]
HASHTAGS: 3–5 (niche-specific, not generic)

Voice: [your authentic professional voice — no buzzwords, no "excited to share"]
Length: 150–300 words (optimal for LinkedIn algorithm)
```

### Prompt 3: Thought Leadership Series

```
Design a 4-week LinkedIn thought leadership series on: [topic]

My perspective: [your unique take on this topic]
Audience: [who you want to reach]
Business goal: [what you want this series to generate]

Week 1: Challenge conventional wisdom ("Most people think X, but actually...")
Week 2: Share a framework or model you use
Week 3: Case study or story that illustrates your point
Week 4: Actionable advice + position statement

For each week: post hook, key message, supporting points, CTA.
Structure each post to build on the previous without requiring it.
End of series: what offer or conversation to invite.
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What LinkedIn content gets the most engagement?

Personal stories with professional lessons consistently outperform pure information sharing. The formula: specific situation + unexpected insight + universal application. Lists and frameworks perform well but feel generic without your personal experience anchoring them. First-person vulnerability about failures and lessons learned gets significantly more engagement than pure success stories.

### How often should I post on LinkedIn?

Three to five times per week for aggressive growth; one to two for steady professional presence. Consistency matters more than frequency — one quality post per week for a year outperforms 10 posts in a month then silence. The algorithm rewards accounts that post consistently.

### Should I post about topics outside my professional expertise?

Occasionally, yes — personal values, life lessons, and non-work interests humanize a professional profile and attract people who align with you beyond your expertise. Keep it at 20% or less of your content. Your audience follows you for your professional perspective; don't dilute it too much with lifestyle content.

### How do I grow my LinkedIn audience without posting constantly?

Comments are the underrated growth lever. Thoughtful, substantive comments on posts from larger accounts in your space expose you to their entire audience. Ten minutes of strategic commenting per day can drive more profile visits and follows than a mediocre post. Comment with your actual opinion, not just "great post."

### What's the LinkedIn algorithm's most important signal?

Early engagement velocity — how many people interact in the first 60–90 minutes after posting. Posts that get strong early engagement get shown to progressively larger audiences. Optimize for this by: posting when your audience is online, ending posts with a question to drive comments, and engaging with every comment you receive in the first hour.
