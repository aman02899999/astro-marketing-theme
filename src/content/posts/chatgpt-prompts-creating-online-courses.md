Creating an online course used to take months. With the right ChatGPT prompts, you can go from idea to complete curriculum draft in days — then spend your time recording, not writing. These prompts cover the entire course creation workflow.

## Course Strategy Prompts

### Prompt 1: Course Idea Validator

```
Help me validate this online course idea:

Course idea: [describe your course concept]
My expertise: [what you know about this topic]
Target student: [who would take this]
Transformation promised: [before state → after state]
Price I'm considering: [$X]
Format: [video / text / live / hybrid]

Analyze this idea across:
1. Market demand signals (what problems this solves, who has them)
2. Competition landscape (what's likely already out there)
3. Unique angle (what would make my version worth paying for)
4. Ideal student profile (most motivated buyers)
5. Potential objections students would have before buying
6. Recommended price point with justification
7. Biggest risk to this idea

Be honest. I'd rather pivot now than launch to silence.
```

---

### Prompt 2: Course Outcome Statement

```
Help me write a clear, compelling course outcome statement for:

Course topic: [topic]
Target student: [who they are]
Their current situation: [where they are before the course]
Their desired situation: [where they want to be]
Timeframe: [how long the course takes]

Write 5 versions of the core promise statement in this format:
"By the end of [course name], you'll be able to [specific, measurable outcome]
— even if [common obstacle or objection]."

Make each version meaningfully different in angle and emphasis.
Rank them by specificity and believability.
```

## Curriculum Design Prompts

### Prompt 3: Full Course Curriculum Outline

```
Create a complete curriculum outline for:

Course title: [title]
Target student: [detailed description]
Course promise: [the transformation]
Format: [video-based / text / workshop]
Total length: [X hours of content]
Number of modules: [X modules]

For each module provide:
- Module title and theme
- Learning objectives (2–3 per module, using Bloom's Taxonomy verbs)
- Lessons within the module (title + 1-sentence description each)
- Estimated lesson length
- Module project or assignment
- Key takeaway (what students remember from this module)

Also provide:
- Course prerequisites
- What's NOT included (scope management)
- Suggested bonus content ideas
```

### Prompt 4: Lesson Script Outline

```
Create a detailed lesson script outline for:

Lesson title: [title]
Module: [which module this belongs to]
Lesson length: [X minutes]
Learning objective: [what students can do after this lesson]
Prior lessons: [what students already know coming in]

Outline structure:
- Hook (first 60 seconds — why this lesson matters right now)
- Recap of previous lesson (30 seconds)
- Core content (broken into 3–5 teaching points)
  For each point: concept → example → application
- Common mistake section (1–2 minutes)
- Lesson summary (key takeaways)
- Bridge to next lesson (what's coming)
- Assignment or action step

Mark where to add: [SCREEN RECORDING], [SLIDE VISUAL], [DEMO], [WORKSHEET REFERENCE]
```

## Student Materials Prompts

### Prompt 5: Worksheet and Workbook Creator

```
Create a student worksheet for:

Lesson: [lesson title]
Learning objective: [what students should accomplish]
Worksheet type: [reflection / exercise / template / checklist / framework]

Include:
1. Lesson summary (3–5 bullet points students fill in)
2. Core exercise (the main activity)
3. Reflection questions (3 questions to deepen learning)
4. Action step (specific thing to do before next lesson)
5. Space for notes

Format: Clean, professional, suitable for PDF download.
Write all instructions in second person, direct and encouraging tone.
```

---

### Prompt 6: Quiz and Knowledge Check Generator

```
Create a lesson quiz for:

Lesson topic: [topic]
Learning objectives covered: [list]
Number of questions: [10–15]
Question types: [multiple choice / true-false / short answer / mixed]

Requirements:
- Questions test understanding, not just memorization
- Plausible wrong answers (distractors should be common misconceptions)
- Mix of difficulty levels: 40% recall / 40% comprehension / 20% application
- Clear, unambiguous wording
- Answer key with brief explanation for each correct answer

After the quiz: provide 3 "extension questions" for advanced students.
```

## Course Launch Prompts

### Prompt 7: Sales Page Copy

```
Write a complete sales page for:

Course name: [name]
Price: [$X]
Target student: [detailed profile]
Core promise: [the transformation]
What's included: [modules, bonuses, format, length]
Social proof available: [testimonials / beta students / your credentials]
Guarantee: [money-back policy]

Sales page sections:
1. Headline + subheadline (outcome-focused)
2. Empathy section (you understand their struggle)
3. Why this course exists (your story/credibility)
4. What they'll learn (module breakdown)
5. Who this is for (and who it's NOT for)
6. What they get (full deliverables list)
7. Social proof block (testimonial templates)
8. Pricing and guarantee
9. FAQ section (8 questions)
10. Final CTA

Tone: Warm, direct, confident. No hype. No "life-changing" promises.
```

### Prompt 8: Launch Email Sequence

```
Write a 7-email launch sequence for my online course:

Course: [name and one-sentence description]
Price: [$X — or early bird $Y until [date]]
Target buyer: [description]
Launch window: [X days open cart]
Key objections: [list the main reasons people hesitate]

Email 1 (Day 1 — Doors open): Announce + full details
Email 2 (Day 2): Address objection #1 with a story
Email 3 (Day 3): Student success story or case study (real or hypothetical)
Email 4 (Day 4): FAQ email (answer 5 common questions)
Email 5 (Day 5): Objection #2 — "Is this right for me?"
Email 6 (Day 6): "Last 24 hours" warning
Email 7 (Day 7 — Final hours): Close + what happens at midnight

Each email: subject line (3 variations), preview text, body, CTA.
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long should an online course be?

As long as it takes to deliver the promised transformation — no longer. Most successful courses are 4–8 hours of content across 4–8 modules. Longer isn't more valuable; completion rate is. Students pay for transformation, not content volume. If your students consistently complete 100% of a 4-hour course, that's more valuable than a 20-hour course with 20% completion.

### What's the best format for an online course in 2025?

Video-based courses with downloadable worksheets remain the most popular and highest-converting format. Add live Q&A calls for premium price points. Text-based courses with strong written content work extremely well for intermediate-to-advanced audiences. Choose the format you'll actually produce consistently.

### How much should I charge for my first online course?

More than you think. $97–$497 is a common range for a self-paced video course. $500–$2,000 for courses with community, live calls, or implementation support. Underpricing signals low quality more often than it increases conversions. Price for the value of the transformation, not the hours of content.

### Can I use ChatGPT scripts to record my course?

Yes, with a critical step: personalize them. Read the script aloud before recording. Rewrite anything that doesn't sound like how you actually talk. Add your own stories, examples, and experiences throughout. The script is a starting point — your voice and expertise make it a course worth buying.

### How do I validate a course idea before building it?

Presell it. Write the sales page (Prompt 7), share it with your audience, and ask for payment before you build anything. If 10 people pay for a course that doesn't exist yet, you have proof of demand. If nobody buys, you've saved weeks of production work on something the market doesn't want.
