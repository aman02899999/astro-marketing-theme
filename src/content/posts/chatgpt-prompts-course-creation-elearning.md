Online courses are one of the highest-margin digital products you can create. These prompts help you go from idea to launched course — covering curriculum design, lesson scripting, sales copy, and student engagement.

## Curriculum Design Prompts

### Prompt 1: Course Curriculum Builder

```
Design a course curriculum for: "[course topic]"

Target student: [who takes this course]
Transformation: [where they start → where they finish]
Course format: [video / text / live cohort / self-paced]
Duration: [X hours of content / X weeks]
Price point: [$X — affects depth expectations]

Curriculum structure:
Module 1: Foundation (what they need to know first)
Module 2–N: Core content (the journey)
Final module: Implementation/project/next steps

For each module:
- Module title and learning objective
- Lesson list (3–7 lessons per module)
- Each lesson: title + one-sentence description + estimated length
- Module project or practice exercise
- Assessment question (to check comprehension)

Final deliverable: what students create or can do by course end.
```

### Prompt 2: Video Lesson Script

```
Write a video lesson script for:

Lesson title: "[title]"
Module: [which module in the course]
Learning objective: [what students will be able to DO after this lesson]
Prior knowledge: [what they've already learned]
Video length target: [X minutes]
Teaching style: [lecture / tutorial / demonstration / story-based]

Script structure:
0:00 HOOK (30 sec): Why this matters — specific scenario
0:30 OBJECTIVE (15 sec): "By the end of this lesson, you'll be able to..."
TEACHING (body): concept → example → application [repeat for each point]
[DEMO MARKER] or [SLIDE NEEDED] throughout
SUMMARY: 3-bullet recap
NEXT STEP: bridge to next lesson

Write the complete word-for-word script with production notes.
```

### Prompt 3: Course Sales Page

```
Write a sales page for my online course:

Course: "[title]"
Target student: [description]
Price: [$X]
Duration: [X hours over X weeks]
Format: [self-paced video / live cohort / etc.]
Key transformation: [before → after]
Social proof: [student count / testimonials / credentials]
Launch or evergreen: [launch date / always available]

Sales page sections:
1. Hero: headline + subheadline + CTA (above the fold)
2. Problem section: where your student is now
3. Solution: introduce the course and the transformation
4. Curriculum overview: module-by-module highlights
5. Who this is for (and who it's NOT for)
6. Instructor credibility (3 sentences)
7. Social proof: testimonials with specific outcomes
8. What's included: the full offer
9. Pricing and CTA
10. FAQ: 6 questions that address final objections
11. Money-back guarantee (if applicable)
12. Final CTA with urgency (if launch)
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long should an online course be?

As long as it takes to achieve the promised transformation — no longer. "Longer = more value" is a persistent myth. Students abandon padded courses and leave negative reviews. A focused 3-hour course with a clear outcome beats a sprawling 15-hour course with filler. Measure by transformation delivered, not hours recorded.

### What's the most important part of a course sales page?

The transformation statement — the specific before and after you promise. "Go from not knowing Python to building your first data pipeline in 4 weeks" is more compelling than "Learn Python programming." The more specific the transformation and the more precisely it matches what the target student wants, the higher the conversion rate.

### Should I create the whole course before launching?

No — use a "minimum viable course" approach. Launch with 20–30% of the content ready, deliver the rest live to your first cohort. This gets real student feedback that improves the course, generates testimonials for future sales, and means you're building based on real needs rather than assumptions. Many successful course creators have launched with just module 1 complete.

### What platform should I use to host my course?

Teachable and Thinkific for self-hosted with built-in marketing tools. Skool for community-integrated courses. Gumroad for simple digital products without LMS features. Kajabi for an all-in-one marketing + course platform. Maven or Cohort-based platforms for live cohort courses. Choose based on your tech comfort, budget, and whether community features matter.

### What conversion rate should I expect on my course sales page?

Cold traffic: 0.5–2%. Warm traffic (your email list, social followers): 2–8%. Launch to a highly engaged, specifically interested list: 5–15%. The biggest variable is audience qualification — people who opted in specifically because of your course topic convert dramatically better than general interest followers.
