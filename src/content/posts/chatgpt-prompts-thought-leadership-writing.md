Thought leadership is the highest-leverage content you can create — it shapes how your entire industry thinks, attracts the best clients and talent, and compounds over years. These prompts help you articulate the ideas you have, in your voice, at a level of quality worth publishing.

## Idea Development Prompts

### Prompt 1: Thought Leadership Idea Generator

```
Help me develop thought leadership ideas in: [your field]

My expertise: [what you know deeply]
My contrarian views: [things you believe that most in your industry don't]
Experiences that shaped my perspective: [specific situations you've lived through]
What I'm frustrated about in my industry: [what's wrong that others accept]
What I've figured out that others haven't: [your hard-won insights]

Generate 10 thought leadership angles:
5 that challenge industry assumptions ("Most [professionals] believe X, but...")
3 that share a framework or model you've developed
2 that make a prediction about where your industry is heading

For each: a working headline and the core argument in 2 sentences.
Rate each by: originality and your specific ability to argue it convincingly.
```

### Prompt 2: Opinion Article / Op-Ed Structure

```
Help me write an opinion article for: [publication type — industry trade / business press / LinkedIn]

My argument: [your specific position]
Why I'm qualified to make it: [relevant experience]
The evidence: [data, examples, or experiences that support it]
The counterargument: [what thoughtful critics would say]
Who I'm writing for: [the specific reader profile]

Article structure:
1. LEDE: the provocative opening (anecdote / surprising fact / direct challenge to conventional wisdom)
2. THESIS: my argument stated clearly (paragraph 2)
3. CASE: 3 supporting arguments with evidence
4. COUNTERARGUMENT: steelman the opposition — then refute it
5. IMPLICATIONS: what follows if I'm right — what should change?
6. CALL TO ACTION: what readers should do / believe / question

Length: 700–1,000 words for most publications.
Tone: confident, evidence-based, intellectually honest.
```

### Prompt 3: Industry Report Introduction

```
Write the introduction section of an industry report for:

Report title: "[title]"
My company/organization: [description]
Report topic: [what it covers]
Key findings I'll present: [list 3–5 headline findings]
Audience: [who reads industry reports in your field]
Year: [current year]

Introduction sections:
1. Executive summary (250 words — standalone, covers all key findings)
2. About this report (methodology, sample size, data sources)
3. Context section (why this topic matters now — market conditions, macro trends)
4. How to use this report (how to navigate the document)
5. Key findings preview (numbered list of 5–7 top-line findings)

Tone: authoritative, data-grounded, forward-looking.
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I develop a genuine point of view rather than regurgitating common knowledge?

Start with your frustrations. The strongest thought leadership comes from practitioners who've lived a problem and found a solution the field doesn't know about yet. Ask yourself: "What do I believe that most people in my industry are wrong about?" That tension is where original perspective lives. Prompts help you structure and articulate it — the perspective itself must be genuinely yours.

### Should I use ChatGPT to write the whole thought leadership piece?

Use it to structure and develop the argument; write the key passages yourself, particularly the opening paragraph and the central argument. Your distinctive voice and specific experiences are what make thought leadership worth reading. An AI-written piece that sounds like a well-organized generalist will never build the kind of authority that a genuine expert voice establishes.

### Where should I publish thought leadership content?

Industry trade publications for deep domain credibility. LinkedIn for professional reach and direct audience building. Business press (Forbes, HBR, Inc.) for broader authority signals. Your own newsletter for relationship depth with your existing audience. Prioritize the publication your target clients or employers actually read.

### How often should I publish thought leadership content?

One strong, original piece per month compounds better than four weak, generic pieces. Thought leadership is about quality and originality, not volume. A single article that genuinely challenges how your field thinks about something can drive inbound opportunities for years.

### How do I get published in industry publications?

Most accept unsolicited pitches. Send a 200-word pitch that describes: your argument, why their readers will care, why you're qualified, and why now. Link to one or two strong writing samples. Follow up once after 2 weeks. Rejection rates are high — pitch multiple outlets simultaneously. Building a strong LinkedIn presence with original posts establishes credibility that makes editors more receptive.
