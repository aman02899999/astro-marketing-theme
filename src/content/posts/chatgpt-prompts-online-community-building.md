Online communities succeed or die in the first 30 days of a member's experience. These prompts help you design that experience deliberately — from the onboarding message to the weekly rituals that keep members coming back.

## Community Setup Prompts

### Prompt 1: Community Onboarding Flow

```
Design an onboarding flow for new members of:

Community: [name and what it's about]
Platform: [Discord / Slack / Circle / Facebook Group / other]
Members join because: [their motivation]
Community goal: [what you want members to achieve]
Current pain: [what's broken in your current onboarding — or "starting from scratch"]

Design the first 7 days:

Day 0 — Welcome:
- Automated welcome DM or channel message (write it)
- First action to take (one simple thing — fills their profile / introduces themselves)

Days 1–3 — Orientation:
- What to explore first
- Where to ask questions
- Introduction prompt (write it — specific enough to get real responses)

Days 4–7 — First contribution:
- How to guide them to post their first piece of value
- Milestone trigger (what earns the "active member" status)

Also: what successful community members do in their first week vs. lurkers who never engage.
```

### Prompt 2: Community Rules and Guidelines

```
Write community rules for:

Community: [name and topic]
Platform: [Discord / Slack / Facebook / etc.]
Tone: [warm / professional / playful / serious]
Primary risk: [spam / negativity / off-topic / self-promotion / misinformation]
Culture we're building: [describe the kind of community]

Rules structure:
1. Community mission (why we exist — 2 sentences)
2. Core behavior rules (5–7 specific, enforceable rules)
3. What's encouraged (not just what's forbidden)
4. Self-promotion policy (if any)
5. Moderation approach (how rules are enforced)
6. How to report issues
7. Appeals process

Write rules that a 10-year-old could follow and a moderator could enforce consistently.
Avoid: vague rules that depend on subjective judgment.
```

## Engagement Prompts

### Prompt 3: Weekly Engagement Prompt Calendar

```
Create a 4-week engagement prompt calendar for a community about: [topic]

Community size: [X members]
Activity level: [very active / moderate / quiet]
Goal: [increase participation / deepen relationships / generate content / all three]
Platform: [where the community lives]

For each day of the week (Mon–Fri), design a recurring prompt type:
Monday: [Motivation/goal-setting related]
Tuesday: [Skill/education share]
Wednesday: [Question/discussion starter]
Thursday: [Showcase/celebrate member work]
Friday: [Reflection/wins celebration]

For each of the 4 weeks, write the specific prompt for each day.
Prompts should:
- Be answerable in 1–3 sentences (low friction)
- Build on the community's shared interest
- Create connection between members (not just to the admin)
```

### Prompt 4: Moderator Response Templates

```
Create moderation response templates for:

Community: [name]
Platform: [Discord / Slack / etc.]
Community tone: [describe]

Templates for:
1. First-time rule violation (gentle redirect)
2. Repeat violation (firm warning)
3. Banning a member (clear, non-antagonistic)
4. Resolving a member conflict (de-escalation)
5. Welcoming a new member who introduces themselves
6. Recognizing a valuable contribution publicly
7. Addressing misinformation or incorrect advice
8. Handling a spam post (delete + response)

Each template:
- Tone: calm, professional, never personal
- 2–3 sentence maximum
- Leaves the door open for good behavior
```

## Growth Strategy Prompts

### Prompt 5: Community Growth Plan

```
Build a 90-day growth plan for:

Community: [name and focus]
Current size: [X members]
Goal: [X members in 90 days]
Current acquisition: [how do members find you now]
Content creator / influencer relationships: [any you have]

90-day plan:
Month 1: Foundation (before growing, make sure it's worth joining)
- Retention metrics to establish first
- Community health indicators to track
- Content/engagement systems to set up

Month 2: Acquisition (grow the top of funnel)
- Primary acquisition channel for this niche
- Content strategy to attract members
- Partnership or collaboration opportunities

Month 3: Amplification (turn members into recruiters)
- Referral mechanic design
- Success story capture process
- Public social proof strategy

Weekly metric to track throughout: [define your North Star metric]
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What platform should I use for my online community?

Discord: best for casual, real-time communities around shared interests (gaming, hobbies, creator fanbases). Slack: best for professional communities with serious discourse. Circle: best for paid communities or course-based communities. Facebook Groups: best when your audience is already on Facebook and not tech-forward. Skool: growing fast for course creators. Choose based on where your audience already spends time, not where you prefer.

### How large does a community need to be before it's "alive"?

A community of 100 highly engaged members beats a dormant community of 10,000. Critical mass for self-sustaining activity is around 150–200 active members (Dunbar's number territory). Before that threshold, you're manually driving most engagement. Use Prompt 3's calendar to maintain artificial momentum until the community reaches organic self-sustainability.

### What's the biggest community building mistake?

Growing before the foundation is solid. Bringing 1,000 people into a community with no clear purpose, no engagement system, and no culture produces noise, not community. Prompt 1 (onboarding) and Prompt 2 (rules) should be in place before you invite anyone. The first 50 members define the culture for everyone who follows.

### How do I handle member conflict professionally?

Address it quickly (silence implies condoning), privately first (don't create public spectacles), and with equal application of rules (no favoritism). Use Prompt 4's conflict resolution template. The goal is resolution, not punishment. Most community conflicts resolve when a calm third party acknowledges both perspectives and redirects to the community's shared purpose.

### Should I charge for my community?

Paid communities typically have higher engagement than free ones because members have self-selected as highly motivated and serious. Free communities reach more people but have more lurkers. The right choice depends on your business model: free if community builds a top-of-funnel audience for a product; paid if community is the product. Both work — vague free communities with no clear value proposition don't.
