LinkedIn is the most powerful B2B platform in the world — and most people use it like a résumé dump. These prompts help you build genuine authority, attract inbound leads, and grow a network that opens doors.

## LinkedIn Post Prompts

### Prompt 1: Thought Leadership Post Formula

```
Write a LinkedIn thought leadership post:

My role: [job title and expertise]
Topic: [specific topic or insight]
Audience: [who follows me / who I want to reach]
Key insight: [the non-obvious thing I want to share]
Evidence: [personal experience / data / observation]

Post structure:
Line 1: Bold opener — hook that challenges assumptions or promises value
Lines 2–10: Build the argument (short paragraphs, maximum 2 sentences each)
Line 11–13: The counterintuitive insight or "here's what I actually learned"
Line 14–15: Universal takeaway
Line 16: Engagement question

Style: Direct. No corporate language. Personal, not institutional.
No: "Excited to share", "humbled", "passionate about", "synergy".
```

### Prompt 2: Personal Story Post

```
Write a LinkedIn personal story post:

Story in one sentence: [what happened]
Lesson: [what it taught you professionally]
Audience: [who on LinkedIn should care about this]

Narrative structure:
1. Start in the middle of the action (not "I remember when...")
2. Build context briefly
3. The turning point or unexpected thing that happened
4. What you realized or learned
5. How this applies to readers in [profession/role]
6. Question for the comments

Tone: Vulnerable but professional. Real, not polished. First-person throughout.
Length: 200–250 words.
```

### Prompt 3: LinkedIn Carousel (Document Post)

```
Write a LinkedIn carousel/document post on: "[topic]"

Audience: [professional role and seniority]
Goal: [reposts / saves / inbound DMs / profile views]
Number of slides: 8–10

For each slide:
- Slide number and role (cover / content / CTA)
- Headline (under 10 words)
- Supporting copy (under 50 words)
- Visual direction [icon / data / illustration type]

Slide 1: Cover — bold title + who this is for
Slides 2–8: One insight or framework step per slide
Slide 9: Key takeaways (3 bullet summary)
Slide 10: CTA — follow + comment ask

Optimize for: saves and shares (make it worth bookmarking).
```

## LinkedIn Profile Prompts

### Prompt 4: LinkedIn Headline Generator

```
Write 10 LinkedIn headline variations for:

Name: [your name]
Current role: [title at company]
Superpower: [what you're best at]
Target audience: [who you want to attract — hiring managers / clients / etc.]
Goal of your LinkedIn: [job search / thought leadership / business development]

Requirements per headline:
- Under 220 characters
- Avoid: "Passionate about", "Guru", "Ninja", "Rockstar"
- Include value proposition, not just job title

Flag the top 3 for each goal (job search vs. business development).
```

### Prompt 5: LinkedIn About Section

```
Write a LinkedIn About section for:

Name and role: [name, title]
Expertise: [what you're known for]
Career highlights: [2–3 specific achievements with numbers]
Who you help: [clients / employers / collaborators]
Your unique approach: [what makes you different]
CTA: [what you want readers to do — connect / DM / visit site]

Format:
- First line: Hook (this appears before "see more" — it must earn the click)
- 3–4 short paragraphs with line breaks
- Bullet achievements section
- Closing CTA line

Tone: First person, conversational, confident — not a résumé in paragraph form.
Length: 250–300 words.
```

## LinkedIn Outreach Prompts

### Prompt 6: Connection Request Messages

```
Write 5 LinkedIn connection request messages for:

Situation: [how I found them / why I'm connecting]
My role: [your title]
Their role: [their title and company]
Goal: [genuine networking / business development / job inquiry]

Requirements:
- Under 300 characters (LinkedIn limit)
- Reference something specific about them
- No immediate pitch
- Make them want to accept

Write 5 variations:
1. Mutual connection reference
2. Their content comment follow-up
3. Same community/event/group
4. Admired their work
5. Cold but respectful professional outreach
```

### Prompt 7: LinkedIn DM Follow-Up Sequence

```
Write a 3-message DM sequence for LinkedIn after someone accepts my connection request:

My product/service: [description]
Their profile suggests they need: [pain point or goal]
My goal: [get a discovery call / share a resource / build relationship first]

Message 1 (Day 1 after connect): Thank + genuine observation, no pitch
Message 2 (Day 5): Value-add — share a resource or insight relevant to them
Message 3 (Day 12): Soft ask — see if there's a fit

Requirements: Under 150 words each. No "just checking in." Each message must stand alone.
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How often should I post on LinkedIn for growth?

Three to five times per week is the sweet spot. Daily posting works if quality is maintained. Consistency for 90 days compounds dramatically — LinkedIn's algorithm heavily rewards established posting patterns.

### What LinkedIn content format gets the most reach?

In 2025, document posts (carousels) consistently outperform other formats for saves and shares. Text-only posts with strong hooks often outperform posts with images for raw reach. Video is growing. Test all formats over 30 days and double down on what your audience responds to.

### How do I build a LinkedIn following from zero?

Start by engaging meaningfully on 5–10 posts per day in your niche before focusing on your own content. Comment with genuine insights (not "Great post!"). Connect with people in your industry strategically. Post 3x/week for 60 days. The compounding effect kicks in around the 500-follower mark.

### Can I use the same post for multiple LinkedIn accounts?

Yes, with meaningful variation. Same core idea, different angle, different examples. Direct duplication (same text on multiple accounts) risks being flagged and produces lower reach since LinkedIn's algorithm detects it.

### What's the ROI of LinkedIn for B2B businesses?

For B2B, LinkedIn generates 80% of social media leads according to LinkedIn's own research. For professional services, consulting, and SaaS, a well-built LinkedIn presence is often the single highest-ROI marketing channel. Invest consistently for 6 months before evaluating results.
