A product launch is the highest-leverage marketing event in your calendar. These prompts help you plan every phase — from pre-launch buzz to post-launch momentum — so you don't leave revenue on the table.

## Launch Planning Prompts

### Prompt 1: Launch Strategy Brief

```
Build a product launch strategy for:

Product: [name and description]
Launch date: [target date]
Audience: [who it's for]
Price point: [$X]
Business goal: [$X revenue / X units / X email signups in launch week]
Channels available: [email list / social / paid ads / partnerships / press]
List/audience size: [X subscribers / followers]
Unique launch angle: [what makes this launch different or time-sensitive]

Strategy document:
1. Pre-launch phase (4 weeks out): build anticipation
2. Soft launch (1 week out): early access or waitlist
3. Launch day: full campaign execution
4. Launch week: sustain momentum
5. Post-launch (weeks 2–4): convert late adopters

For each phase: specific content, emails, and posts with timing.
```

---

### Prompt 2: Launch Email Sequence

```
Write a product launch email sequence for:

Product: [name and what it does]
Audience: [email list description — warm / cold / segmented]
Launch date: [date]
Price: [$X, and any launch pricing or bonuses]
Sequence length: [X emails over X days]

Email sequence:
Email 1 (7 days before): Problem awareness — don't mention the product
Email 2 (5 days before): Solution exists — tease without revealing
Email 3 (3 days before): Product reveal + open waitlist/early access
Email 4 (1 day before): Launch reminder + urgency (bonus/deadline)
Email 5 (Launch day): It's here — full offer, CTA
Email 6 (Day 3): Social proof — early results, testimonials
Email 7 (Final day): Last chance — deadline reminder

Each email: subject line (3 options), preview text, body copy, CTA.
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How far in advance should I plan a product launch?

Minimum 8 weeks for a digital product launch; 12–16 weeks for a physical product or major software release. The pre-launch phase (building anticipation, collecting leads, testing messaging) is where most revenue is actually determined. Rushed launches almost always underperform because there's no warmed-up audience.

### What's the most important launch metric?

Email list size and engagement rate of people interested in the product — not total followers. A list of 1,000 highly engaged, product-fit subscribers outperforms 100,000 disengaged followers for conversion. Every launch plan should include a mechanism for building a specific interest list before launch day.

### How do I create urgency without being manipulative?

Use real deadlines and real scarcity. Launch pricing that genuinely expires. Limited bonuses that genuinely disappear. A founding member tier that genuinely closes. Manufactured urgency ("Only 3 left!" when you have infinite digital copies) trains your audience not to trust you. Genuine scarcity converts better long-term.

### What's a good launch conversion rate?

For a warm email list of interested subscribers: 2–5% is typical, 5–10% is strong, 10%+ is exceptional. For cold audiences, expect 0.5–2%. These vary significantly by price point, product fit, and launch execution quality. Track your own baselines and improve each launch.

### Should I do a soft launch before the main launch?

Yes — a beta, early access, or founder pricing tier before full launch serves multiple purposes: generate revenue before full launch, collect testimonials for social proof, identify product issues, and reward your most engaged audience. Prompt 1's "soft launch phase" includes this structure.
