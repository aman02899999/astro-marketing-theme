YouTube is the world's second-largest search engine. Every video you publish is either optimized to be found or invisible. These prompts apply SEO principles specifically to YouTube — from keyword research to full metadata optimization.

## Keyword Research Prompts

### Prompt 1: YouTube Keyword Strategy

```
Build a YouTube keyword strategy for a channel about: [your niche]

Channel focus: [specific sub-niche]
Audience: [who watches]
Competition level: [new channel / established]

Identify keyword categories:

HIGH VOLUME (hard to rank, good for established channels):
- [5 broad keywords in your niche]

MEDIUM VOLUME, LOW COMPETITION (best for growing channels):
- [10 specific how-to and tutorial keywords]

LONG-TAIL (easiest to rank, niche audience):
- [15 very specific question-based keywords]

TRENDING (time-sensitive opportunity):
- [5 keywords tied to current trends in your niche]

For each keyword estimate:
- Search intent (tutorial / comparison / review / inspiration)
- Content format that ranks best (long tutorial / short explainer / listicle / vlog)
- Suggested video title
```

### Prompt 2: Competitor Video Analysis

```
Analyze these top-ranking YouTube videos for the keyword: "[keyword]"

Videos to analyze (describe or list titles):
1. [Video 1 title and rough description]
2. [Video 2 title and roughly]
3. [Video 3 title and roughly]

Analyze:
1. Title patterns — what makes them work?
2. Thumbnail patterns — what visual elements appear consistently?
3. Estimated video length for this keyword
4. Topics they cover (content gaps I could fill)
5. Comment section signals (what viewers want that wasn't addressed)
6. My differentiation angle — how my video on this topic will be different/better

Output: a brief to create a video that will outperform these results.
```

## Metadata Optimization Prompts

### Prompt 3: Complete Video Metadata Package

```
Write complete YouTube metadata for:

Video topic: [what the video covers]
Primary keyword: "[keyword]"
Secondary keywords: [3–5 related terms]
Video length: [X minutes]
Key points covered: [list main sections]
Channel name: [name]

Produce:

TITLE (3 options):
- Option A: keyword-forward
- Option B: benefit/outcome-forward
- Option C: curiosity/intrigue-forward
Each under 60 characters.

DESCRIPTION (full):
Paragraph 1 (above the fold — 125 chars): hook + keyword
Paragraph 2–3: detailed video summary with secondary keywords
Chapters: (template format — timestamps to fill in)
Links section: (placeholders)
About the channel: (2 sentences)
Hashtags: 3–5 at bottom

TAGS: 15 tags ranked by priority (mix of exact match, broad, and related)

END SCREEN CTA SCRIPT: 15-second spoken outro
```

### Prompt 4: Chapter Titles Optimizer

```
Write YouTube chapter titles for this video:

Video title: "[title]"
Video topic: [description]
My rough chapter breakdown:
[paste your timestamps and rough section descriptions]

Optimize chapter titles to:
1. Include secondary keywords where natural
2. Create curiosity/value hooks (not just descriptive labels)
3. Follow a logical flow that implies a complete learning journey
4. Be short enough to display fully (under 40 characters)

Format:
00:00 [Chapter title]
03:22 [Chapter title]
[etc.]

Also: how should I phrase the "00:00 Introduction" to make it compelling vs. generic?
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do YouTube tags still matter for SEO?

Less than they used to. YouTube's algorithm now relies more heavily on the title, description, and transcript for understanding video content. That said, tags still help YouTube understand context and surface your video in the "suggested videos" sidebar alongside related content. Include 10–15 relevant tags mixing broad and specific terms, but don't obsess over them.

### What's the most important YouTube SEO factor?

Click-through rate (CTR) and watch time together. If your title and thumbnail earn clicks, and viewers watch most of your video, YouTube's algorithm amplifies your reach. SEO metadata (title, description, tags) gets your video into initial search results; viewer behavior determines whether it stays and gets recommended. Optimize for both.

### How long should YouTube video descriptions be?

At least 200–300 words for SEO value. The first 125 characters appear before "Show more" and must include your primary keyword and a compelling hook. After that: expand naturally, include secondary keywords, add your chapter links, and put your resource links and channel boilerplate at the bottom. Use Prompt 3's structure.

### Should I optimize old YouTube videos?

Yes — this is one of the highest-ROI YouTube SEO actions for established channels. Go through your most-viewed videos and update titles, descriptions, and chapters using these prompts. YouTube re-evaluates video SEO when you update metadata, and many creators see significant ranking improvements from refreshing 2–3 year old videos.

### How do hashtags work on YouTube?

YouTube displays up to 3 hashtags above the video title (the first 3 you include). They're clickable and lead to a feed of videos with the same hashtag. Use 3–5 total: your channel hashtag, a niche hashtag, and a topic hashtag. Don't use trending hashtags irrelevant to your video — YouTube penalizes hashtag misuse.
