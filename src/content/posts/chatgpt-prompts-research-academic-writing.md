> **Academic integrity note:** ChatGPT is a research and writing assistant. Using it to produce work you submit as entirely your own, without disclosure, may violate your institution's academic integrity policies. Check your institution's AI use policy before using these prompts for academic submissions.

Research and academic writing have specific conventions, rigor requirements, and structure that general writing prompts don't address. These prompts are designed for the specific challenges of scholarly work.

## Literature and Research Prompts

### Prompt 1: Literature Review Organizer

```
Help me organize a literature review on: [topic]

Papers I'm working with (describe or list):
[List key papers — author, year, main finding]

Review purpose: [thesis chapter / journal article / grant proposal]
Research question: [your specific question]
Scope: [date range, disciplines, types of studies]

Organize the literature by:
1. THEMATIC ORGANIZATION: [group papers by argument, not author]
   - Theme 1: papers that argue [position]
   - Theme 2: papers that argue [contrasting position]
   - Theme 3: papers that focus on [methodological approach]
2. CHRONOLOGICAL DEVELOPMENT: how the field has evolved
3. GAPS: what this body of literature has not addressed
4. YOUR POSITIONING: where your research fits and why it matters

Output: a literature review outline I can write from (not the review itself).
```

### Prompt 2: Argument Developer

```
Help me develop this academic argument:

My claim: [your thesis or argument]
Evidence I have: [list key sources and findings that support it]
Counterarguments in the literature: [what scholars argue against this]
My field: [discipline]
Purpose: [journal article / thesis / conference paper]

Develop the argument structure:
1. STATE the claim precisely (no hedging, no vagueness)
2. GROUND it in the literature (which established scholars/frameworks support it)
3. BUILD the case (logical progression of sub-arguments)
4. ACKNOWLEDGE counterarguments (engage seriously, don't dismiss)
5. REFUTE or QUALIFY: where counterarguments fail, where you concede
6. IMPLICATION: what follows from this argument being correct

Academic standard: every claim needs a warrant (why the evidence supports the claim, not just what the evidence says).
```

### Prompt 3: Methods Section Writer

```
Write a draft methods section for:

Research type: [quantitative / qualitative / mixed methods / systematic review]
Study design: [experiment / survey / case study / interview / meta-analysis / etc.]
Sample: [who/what was studied, how selected, N=]
Data collection: [instruments, procedures, timing]
Analysis approach: [statistical tests / thematic analysis / coding scheme / etc.]
Ethics: [IRB approval / consent procedures]
Limitations: [known methodological constraints]

Methods section requirements:
1. Enough detail for replication (another researcher could repeat this)
2. Justify key methodological choices (why this design for this question)
3. Acknowledge limitations without undermining the study
4. Past tense for completed studies, future for proposals
5. APA/AMA/Chicago format as relevant to my field

Draft the section in [your citation style].
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How should I use ChatGPT in academic writing without plagiarizing?

Use it for: organizing your own ideas, explaining concepts you don't understand, improving clarity of prose you've written, checking argument structure, and suggesting literature you may have missed. Don't use it to generate the substance of your arguments or findings — those must come from your own research. The line is: ChatGPT shapes how you communicate your work; it doesn't do the intellectual work.

### Can ChatGPT help with literature reviews?

For organizing existing literature you've already read: yes, extremely well. For discovering what literature exists: use Google Scholar, PubMed, or JSTOR first, then use ChatGPT to help organize and synthesize what you find. Never cite a paper you haven't actually read — ChatGPT sometimes generates plausible-sounding but non-existent citations (hallucinations).

### What's the most common academic writing mistake?

Stating what a source says without explaining why it supports your argument. "Smith (2019) argues that X" is incomplete. "Smith (2019) demonstrates X, which supports my claim that Y because [the logical connection]" is academic argument. The warrant — the logical bridge between evidence and claim — is what separates good academic writing from literature summary.

### How do I overcome academic writing blocks?

Write ugly first drafts. Academic writing blocks often come from trying to write publishable prose immediately. Use "ugly draft" mode: write your argument in casual language first, get it on paper completely, then revise for academic register. ChatGPT can help clean up the register and formality after you've captured the substance.

### How do I know if my argument is strong enough?

Test it with the strongest possible counterargument (Prompt 2 includes this). If you can't articulate the best case against your own argument and explain why your argument still holds, it's not strong enough yet. The quality of your acknowledgment and rebuttal of counterarguments is as important as the positive case you make.
