Your career is the longest project you'll ever work on. These prompts help you be strategic about it — from writing a resume that gets interviews to negotiating a promotion you deserve.

## Resume Prompts

### Prompt 1: Resume Bullet Point Optimizer

```
Rewrite these resume bullet points to be stronger:

My role: [job title]
My bullets (current):
1. [current bullet]
2. [current bullet]
3. [current bullet]
4. [current bullet]
5. [current bullet]

Improve each bullet to:
- Start with a strong action verb (not "Responsible for" or "Helped")
- Include a quantifiable result where possible (%, $, time saved, scale)
- Show impact, not just activity
- Be under 20 words each
- Pass the "so what?" test

Also: for bullets where I haven't provided numbers, suggest what I should go back and measure or estimate.
```

### Prompt 2: ATS-Optimized Resume

```
Optimize my resume for this job posting:

JOB POSTING:
[paste the full job description]

MY CURRENT RESUME:
[paste your resume]

1. Identify: keywords in the job description that appear in my resume vs. missing
2. Suggest: specific sections or bullets to rewrite to better match the role
3. Recommend: which of my experiences to highlight more prominently
4. Flag: anything in my resume that's irrelevant to this specific role (should be cut)
5. Rewrite: my professional summary to target this exact role

Do not fabricate experience. Only use what I've provided.
```

## Interview Preparation Prompts

### Prompt 3: Behavioral Interview Answer Builder

```
Help me answer this behavioral interview question:

Question: "[e.g. Tell me about a time you handled a difficult stakeholder]"
Role I'm interviewing for: [title and company type]

My relevant experience: [describe a relevant situation — rough notes are fine]

Structure my answer using STAR format:
S — Situation: [context, brief and specific]
T — Task: [what you were responsible for]
A — Action: [what YOU specifically did — not "we"]
R — Result: [measurable outcome + what you learned]

Requirements:
- 90–120 seconds when spoken aloud
- Focus on MY actions, not team or circumstance
- End with a forward-looking statement or lesson
- Avoid filler words and hedging language

Polish my rough notes into a clear, compelling answer.
```

### Prompt 4: Interview Question Anticipator

```
Anticipate interview questions for:

Role: [job title and company]
Job description key requirements: [list 5–7]
My background: [brief description of your experience]
Potential weaknesses in my candidacy: [gaps, job changes, level mismatch]

Generate:
1. Top 10 competency questions for this specific role
2. 3 questions about my background that might be challenging
3. 5 culture/values questions for this company type
4. 2 unexpected questions that might catch me off guard
5. 3 questions I should ask THEM at the end

For each of my challenging questions: suggest how to address it honestly and positively.
```

## Promotion and Career Growth Prompts

### Prompt 5: Promotion Request Builder

```
Help me make the case for a promotion:

My current role: [title]
Role I'm seeking: [title or "next level"]
Time in current role: [X months/years]
Key accomplishments since last review: [list 4–6 with numbers where possible]
How I've grown beyond my current level: [specific examples]
Market rate for my target level: [if known]
My manager's likely concerns: [budget / timing / precedent / your readiness]

Build my case with:
1. Opening statement (why now, why me — confident, not demanding)
2. Accomplishments narrative (organized by business impact, not task)
3. Evidence I'm already performing at the next level
4. Market data framing (if applicable)
5. Response to likely objections
6. Clear ask (specific role and compensation target)
7. What I'm proposing as success metrics for the next 6 months

Format as talking points for a 20-minute conversation.
```

### Prompt 6: 90-Day New Job Plan

```
Create a 90-day plan for starting a new role:

Role: [job title]
Company: [brief description]
My background: [what I bring in]
Key goals for this role: [what success looks like]
Team I'm joining: [size and structure]
Key stakeholders: [who I need to influence]

30/60/90 day plan:

First 30 days (Listen and learn):
- Priorities
- Key relationships to build
- Information to gather
- What NOT to do yet

Days 31–60 (Start contributing):
- First projects or initiatives to own
- Credibility builders
- Systems to understand and improve

Days 61–90 (Deliver and establish):
- Visible wins to achieve
- Long-term roadmap to draft
- How to position for the next opportunity
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I quantify accomplishments I can't measure precisely?

Estimate, then say so: "approximately," "roughly," or "around." "Reduced onboarding time by approximately 30%" is better than "improved onboarding" and completely legitimate. If you genuinely can't estimate, use scale: "handled 50+ customer escalations per quarter" or "managed a $400K marketing budget." Numbers aren't just about precision — they demonstrate that you think in terms of impact.

### Should my resume be one page or two?

One page if under 10 years of experience. Two pages if you have 10+ years of highly relevant experience. Never two pages just because you listed everything — two pages with padding is worse than one tight page. Every line on your resume must earn its place by showing something relevant to the target role.

### How often should I update my resume?

Keep a running "accomplishments document" that you update monthly — note wins, numbers, and projects as they happen while they're fresh. Then update your actual resume quarterly, or immediately when a new opportunity arises. Trying to remember 2-year-old accomplishments when you need a resume is why people undersell themselves.

### How do I ask for a promotion when there's no open role above me?

Make the case for creating the role, or ask for the title even if the role definition stays the same. Frame it as: "I've been performing at [next level] for [X months]. I'd like to formalize that in my title and compensation, and I want to understand what would need to be true for that to happen." This shifts the conversation from "promote me" to "what does the path look like."

### What's the best way to prepare for a case interview?

Practice frameworks: MECE structuring, the 3Cs/4Ps for business cases, market sizing with round numbers. Then practice out loud — the biggest case interview mistake is doing mental math and structuring silently. ChatGPT can run mock case interviews with you: ask it to give you a business case and then evaluate your structure and recommendation.
