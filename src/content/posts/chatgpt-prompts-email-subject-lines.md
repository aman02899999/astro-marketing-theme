Your subject line is the only thing standing between your email and the trash. The average inbox receives 121 emails per day — yours has under 2 seconds to earn a click. These prompts generate subject lines that outperform every time.

## Subject Line Formulas

### Prompt 1: The Subject Line Generator

```
Generate 25 email subject lines for:

Email about: [what the email contains]
Audience: [who receives it]
Goal: [open / click / reply / purchase]
Tone: [professional / casual / urgent / curious]
Brand voice: [how you usually communicate]

Generate 5 subject lines in each of these styles:

1. CURIOSITY GAP ("The one thing most [audience] get wrong about [topic]")
2. DIRECT BENEFIT ("[Specific outcome] in [timeframe]")
3. SOCIAL PROOF ("How [number/name] achieved [result]")
4. QUESTION ("Are you making this [topic] mistake?")
5. URGENCY/SCARCITY (genuine only — "Ends tonight:" / "Last [X] spots:")

For each: predicted open rate vs. average and why it would work.
Flag top 3 to A/B test first.
```

### Prompt 2: Subject Line A/B Test Designer

```
Design an A/B test for email subject lines:

Email content: [brief description]
List segment: [who receives this]
Current average open rate: [X%]
Testing goal: [increase open rate by X% / understand what resonates with this segment]

Test design:
- Version A: [subject line 1 — control or hypothesis 1]
- Version B: [subject line 2 — hypothesis 2]

Variables to test (one at a time):
- Personalization (with vs. without first name)
- Length (short vs. long)
- Emoji (yes vs. no)
- Curiosity vs. direct benefit
- Question vs. statement

For each test: hypothesis, what a win looks like, minimum sample size for significance, how long to run.
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What subject line length works best?

Mobile email clients display 30–40 characters. Desktop shows 60–80. Since 60%+ of email is opened on mobile, front-load your most important words. Under 50 characters wins in most mobile-heavy audiences. Exception: long, specific subject lines sometimes perform well in B2B and newsletter contexts where readers expect detail.

### Do emojis help or hurt open rates?

It depends on your audience and brand. For casual consumer brands, relevant emojis modestly improve open rates. For B2B and professional contexts, they often hurt. Never use emojis in subject lines without A/B testing for your specific list. The emoji should add meaning, not decoration.

### What's the biggest subject line mistake?

Click-bait that doesn't deliver. "You won't believe this..." followed by ordinary content trains subscribers to ignore your subject lines. Subject lines earn trust or destroy it — consistently deliver on the promise in your subject line and your open rates compound over time.

### How do I avoid the spam folder?

Avoid: all caps, excessive exclamation points, spam trigger words ("FREE!!!," "ACT NOW," "GUARANTEED"), misleading subject lines, and image-heavy emails with little text. More importantly: build a clean list (confirmed opt-ins), maintain good sender reputation, and make it genuinely easy to unsubscribe.

### How often should I test subject lines?

Every broadcast email is a test opportunity. Build a subject line swipe file from your best performers — the patterns that work for your audience become your templates. After 20–30 tests you'll have a clear picture of what your specific subscribers respond to.
