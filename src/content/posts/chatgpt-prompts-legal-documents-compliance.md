> **Important disclaimer:** ChatGPT-generated legal documents are starting points for discussion with a qualified attorney, not final legal instruments. Laws vary by jurisdiction. Always have important legal documents reviewed by a licensed attorney before signing or publishing.

Legal document drafting is time-consuming and expensive when done entirely by lawyers. Using ChatGPT to produce a working draft before attorney review can reduce billable hours significantly — if you know how to prompt it correctly.

## Contract Drafting Prompts

### Prompt 1: NDA (Non-Disclosure Agreement)

```
Draft a Non-Disclosure Agreement for:

Parties: [Company A (disclosing party)] and [Company B (receiving party)]
Purpose: [why information is being shared — partnership discussion / vendor evaluation / employment]
Jurisdiction: [state/country for governing law]
Duration: [how long the NDA remains in effect]
Type: [mutual / one-way]
Scope of confidential information: [what types of information are covered]
Exclusions: [what is NOT confidential — publicly available / independently developed]

Include:
1. Definitions of confidential information
2. Obligations of the receiving party
3. Permitted disclosures (employees, advisors on need-to-know basis)
4. Return or destruction of information upon request
5. Term and termination
6. Remedies (acknowledging damages may be inadequate)
7. Governing law and jurisdiction
8. Signature blocks

Plain English — professional but readable. Flag where we need to fill in specific details.
```

### Prompt 2: Service Agreement / Freelance Contract

```
Draft a service agreement between:

Service provider: [name/company, role]
Client: [name/company]
Services: [describe exactly what will be delivered]
Timeline: [project duration and key milestones]
Payment: [$X total / $X per hour / milestone-based schedule]
Intellectual property: [who owns the deliverables]
Confidentiality: [any NDAs needed]
Revision policy: [X rounds of revisions included]
Termination: [notice period and kill fee]
Jurisdiction: [state/country]

Include:
1. Scope of work (with exclusions to prevent scope creep)
2. Payment terms and late payment penalties
3. IP ownership and licensing
4. Warranties and limitations of liability
5. Indemnification
6. Termination clauses
7. Dispute resolution

Note all sections where specific values need to be inserted.
```

## Policy Document Prompts

### Prompt 3: Privacy Policy Generator

```
Draft a privacy policy for:

Company: [name]
Website/app: [URL or description]
Data collected: [list — name, email, payment info, usage data, cookies, etc.]
How data is used: [marketing / service delivery / analytics / etc.]
Third parties data is shared with: [payment processors / analytics / advertising]
Data retention period: [how long you keep data]
User rights: [access / deletion / portability / opt-out]
Jurisdiction: [country — determines legal requirements: GDPR / CCPA / etc.]
Contact: [privacy contact email]

Structure:
1. Introduction and who we are
2. Information we collect (and how)
3. How we use your information
4. How we share your information
5. Cookies and tracking
6. Data retention
7. Your rights and choices
8. Security measures
9. Children's privacy
10. Changes to this policy
11. Contact information

Plain language. No legalese that nobody reads.
```

### Prompt 4: Terms of Service

```
Draft Terms of Service for:

Platform type: [SaaS / marketplace / content site / e-commerce / app]
Company: [name and jurisdiction]
Key risks to address: [user-generated content / transactions / subscriptions / data use]
Payment terms (if applicable): [billing, refunds, cancellation]
Prohibited conduct: [what users cannot do]
Liability limitations: [what you won't be responsible for]
Dispute resolution: [arbitration / court / jurisdiction]

Sections:
1. Acceptance of terms
2. Account registration and eligibility
3. Permitted and prohibited use
4. Intellectual property (yours and users')
5. Payment terms (if applicable)
6. Privacy reference
7. Disclaimers and limitation of liability
8. Indemnification
9. Termination
10. Dispute resolution and governing law
11. Changes to terms
12. Contact

Tone: clear and professional. Avoid making every section feel like a legal threat.
```

## Compliance Prompts

### Prompt 5: Business Compliance Checklist

```
Generate a compliance checklist for:

Business type: [what your business does]
Stage: [startup / established]
Jurisdiction: [country + state/province]
Industry: [regulated industries: finance / health / food / etc. — or "general"]
Data you handle: [customer data / payment data / health data / etc.]
Number of employees: [X]

Checklist areas:
1. Business registration and licensing
2. Tax obligations
3. Employment law requirements
4. Data protection and privacy (GDPR / CCPA / etc.)
5. Industry-specific regulations
6. Contracts and agreements to have in place
7. Insurance requirements
8. Website compliance (privacy policy / cookie consent / accessibility)
9. Intellectual property protection
10. Annual compliance calendar

For each item: priority (critical / important / recommended) and who is responsible.

Note: this is a starting point for discussion with a business attorney and accountant.
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I use ChatGPT-generated contracts without a lawyer?

For very low-stakes agreements between parties with aligned interests, a ChatGPT-drafted agreement reviewed by both sides may be sufficient. For any agreement involving significant money, IP rights, liability, or ongoing relationships, have a licensed attorney review — the cost of a 1-hour legal review is trivial compared to the cost of a disputed contract.

### Are ChatGPT-generated contracts legally binding?

A contract is binding based on its substance (offer, acceptance, consideration, mutual intent) — not its origin. A ChatGPT-drafted contract that both parties sign with full understanding can be legally binding. However, poorly drafted clauses may be unenforceable, ambiguous, or miss jurisdiction-specific requirements. Legal review ensures enforceability.

### What's the most important clause in a service agreement?

Scope of work and IP ownership together. Scope prevents disputes about what was and wasn't included. IP ownership determines who controls the deliverables long-term — especially critical for creative work, software, and anything with commercial value. These two clauses cause the most disputes in freelance and agency agreements.

### How do I handle GDPR compliance as a small business?

Key requirements: (1) have a lawful basis for every type of data you collect, (2) publish a privacy policy (Prompt 3), (3) enable users to request their data and deletion, (4) implement reasonable security measures, (5) if you have EU customers, potentially appoint a data protection officer. Start with the privacy policy and a clear data inventory (what you collect and why).

### How often should I update my privacy policy and terms of service?

Review at minimum annually, and immediately when: you add new data collection, change how you use data, add new third-party services, expand to new jurisdictions, or change your business model. Add a version date to all policy documents. Notify users of material changes via email or in-app notice.
