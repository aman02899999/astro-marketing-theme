Sales is fundamentally about having the right conversation at the right time. These prompts help you have more of those conversations — by making outreach sharper, discovery more structured, and follow-up more consistent.

## Prospecting Prompts

### Prompt 1: Cold Outreach Email Sequence

```
Write a cold outreach sequence for:

My product/service: [description and ICP — ideal customer profile]
Prospect: [role, company type, pain I solve for them]
Differentiation: [why us vs. alternatives]
Sequence: [3 emails over 2 weeks]

Email 1 (Day 1 — cold intro):
- One personalized reference (their company / recent news / their content)
- Lead with their problem, not my solution
- One question — invite a reply, not a demo request
- Under 100 words
- Subject: [3 options]

Email 2 (Day 5 — value add):
- Share one relevant insight, case study, or stat
- No ask — just value
- Very short (under 75 words)

Email 3 (Day 12 — last touch):
- Acknowledge this is my last outreach
- One final compelling angle
- Clear ask (15-minute call)
- Break-up framing that keeps door open
```

### Prompt 2: Discovery Call Script

```
Write a discovery call script for:

My product: [what it does]
Prospect: [role and company type]
Call duration: [X minutes]
Goal: [qualify / understand pain / build relationship / advance to demo]

Script structure:
1. Opening (2 min): rapport + set the agenda + gain buy-in
2. Situation questions (5 min): understand their current state
3. Problem questions (5 min): uncover pains and frustrations
4. Implication questions (3 min): what does the problem cost them?
5. Need-payoff questions (3 min): what would solving it mean?
6. Next step close (2 min): advance the deal

For each section: exact questions to ask and what a good answer reveals.
Note: this follows SPIN Selling framework (Situation/Problem/Implication/Need-Payoff).
```

### Prompt 3: Objection Handler Library

```
Build an objection handler library for selling: [product/service]

Common objections:
1. "It's too expensive"
2. "We're already using [competitor]"
3. "Now isn't a good time"
4. "We need to think about it"
5. "I need to get budget approval"
6. "Send me more information" [delay tactic]
7. "We handle this in-house"
8. [Add your industry-specific objection]

For each objection:
- What this objection usually REALLY means (beneath the surface)
- Acknowledge (don't argue or dismiss)
- Clarify (dig into the real concern)
- Respond (address the root cause)
- Confirm (check if you've resolved it)

Format as a quick-reference card a rep can review before calls.
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What's the biggest mistake sales reps make on cold outreach?

Pitching too early. The first email should open a conversation, not present a solution. Prompt 1's sequence deliberately withholds the pitch in Email 1 — the goal is a reply, not a demo request. Prospects aren't ready to evaluate a solution they didn't know existed 30 seconds ago.

### How do I personalize cold emails at scale?

Identify one personalizable field per prospect (recent company news, LinkedIn post, mutual connection, specific pain point for their industry). ChatGPT can generate 10 personalization variations around one theme that you mix and match. True personalization doesn't require writing every email from scratch — it requires one specific, relevant reference that shows you did your homework.

### What's the most important discovery call question?

"What happens if you don't solve this?" (the implication question in SPIN). This surfaces the cost of inaction, which is the real competitor in every deal. Prospects often don't act not because your product is wrong, but because the pain isn't painful enough yet. Making the cost of inaction concrete is the most important thing a discovery call can accomplish.

### How do I handle a prospect who won't give a clear answer?

Ask a forced-choice question: "Would it be more helpful to schedule a demo now, or should I follow up next quarter?" This moves from open-ended ambiguity to a binary decision. Many stalls are just prospects not knowing what their next step should be — give them two specific options to choose from.

### What's the right cadence for follow-up?

For warm prospects (had a call): within 24 hours with agreed next steps, then on the schedule you agreed. For cold prospects (no response): Day 1, Day 5, Day 12, Day 21, Day 30+. After 5 contacts with no response, move to a "breakup email" (Prompt 1, Email 3) that closes the loop while leaving the door open for when timing changes.
