Individual blog posts don't rank long-term. Sites with strategic content architecture — pillar pages, topic clusters, and intelligent internal linking — do. These prompts help you build the content system that compounds search rankings over time.

## Content Architecture Prompts

### Prompt 1: Topic Cluster Strategy

```
Build a topic cluster strategy for a site about: [niche]

Domain: [new / established — how old is the site]
Goal: [rank for [keyword] / dominate [sub-niche] / build topical authority]
Content team size: [articles per month you can produce]

Design the cluster architecture:

PILLAR PAGE (one per cluster):
- Title and target keyword (high volume, broad)
- Structure outline (H2s that define the subtopics)
- Word count target: [2,500–5,000 words]
- Internal link destinations (cluster articles that link back to this)

CLUSTER ARTICLES (8–15 per pillar):
For each:
- Title and long-tail keyword
- Brief description (2 sentences)
- Connection to pillar (how it links back)
- Unique angle (what this article covers that the pillar doesn't)

Design 3 complete topic clusters for my site.
Priority order: which to build first for fastest authority gains?
```

### Prompt 2: Content Gap Analysis

```
Identify content gaps for a site about: [niche]

My current content (list URLs or topics covered):
[paste your existing content list]

Competitors to analyze (describe their content scope):
Competitor 1: [description of their content]
Competitor 2: [description of their content]
Competitor 3: [description of their content]

Identify:
1. Topics my competitors cover that I don't (immediate gap)
2. Topics nobody in my niche covers well (opportunity gap)
3. Topics I cover but need to update or expand (optimization gap)
4. Questions my audience asks that no one answers well (FAQ gap)
5. Long-tail keywords with decent volume but low competition (quick win gap)

Output: prioritized list of content to create in the next 90 days, ordered by: [traffic potential × competition difficulty ratio].
```

### Prompt 3: 12-Month Content Roadmap

```
Build a 12-month SEO content roadmap for: [site topic]

Current state:
- Domain authority: [estimate or "new site"]
- Current monthly traffic: [X visitors or "none"]
- Content published: [X articles]
- Target keywords I want to rank for: [list 5–10]

Resources:
- Articles per month: [X]
- Word count average: [X words per article]
- Budget for content: [$X/month or "in-house only"]

Roadmap priorities:
Month 1–3: Foundation (pillar pages and core cluster content)
Month 4–6: Expansion (second-tier clusters and supporting content)
Month 7–9: Authority (data-driven linkable assets, research content)
Month 10–12: Optimization (update top performers, fill remaining gaps)

For each quarter: specific content priorities with rationale.
Milestone targets: where should I be at the end of each quarter?
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does SEO content strategy take to show results?

New sites: 6–12 months before meaningful organic traffic. Established sites with existing authority: 2–4 months for targeted content to start ranking. Google rewards consistency over time — a 12-month roadmap with consistent execution outperforms 6 months of intensity followed by nothing.

### How many cluster articles do I need per pillar page?

8–15 per pillar is a common target. The exact number matters less than coverage quality — you need to cover the topic more comprehensively than competitors, answer the questions your audience actually asks, and link everything together with a clear internal link structure. Thin coverage across 20 articles is worse than deep coverage across 8.

### What's the difference between a pillar page and a cluster article?

Pillar pages target broad, high-volume head terms and cover a topic comprehensively (2,500–5,000+ words). They link out to cluster articles for more detail on each subtopic. Cluster articles target long-tail keywords on specific subtopics and link back to the pillar. This two-way linking structure signals topical authority to Google.

### Should I update old content or create new content?

Both — but updating consistently outperforms most new content creation for established sites. An existing page with rankings, backlinks, and history that gets refreshed with new information often jumps significantly in rankings. Start with your top 20 pages by existing traffic and update those before creating new content on the same topics.

### How do I do keyword research without a paid tool?

Google Search itself: note autocomplete suggestions, People Also Ask boxes, and related searches at the bottom of results pages. Google Search Console: shows what you already rank for (data mine for low-hanging fruit). Answerthepublic.com: free question-based keyword research. These free sources cover 80% of what a paid tool adds for most small sites.
