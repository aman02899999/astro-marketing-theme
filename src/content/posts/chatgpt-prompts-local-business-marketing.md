Local businesses that dominate their market combine online visibility (Google, Maps) with genuine community relationships. These prompts build both — from optimized Google Business Profiles to local content that positions you as the area expert.

## Google Business Profile Prompts

### Prompt 1: Google Business Profile Optimizer

```
Optimize our Google Business Profile for:

Business: [name and type]
Location: [city and neighborhood]
Primary services: [list 5–8]
Differentiators: [what makes you different from local competitors]
Target customers: [who you serve]
Hours and contact: [details to include]

Optimize:
1. Business description (750 character limit):
   - First 250 chars: primary service + location + main differentiator
   - Middle: service list with location keywords
   - End: CTA (call / visit / book)
2. Services section: titles and descriptions for each service
3. Post ideas (weekly posts to keep profile active):
   - 4 post templates (offer / event / product / general update)
4. Q&A seeds (5 questions to add + answers — prevents bad questions appearing first)
5. Photo guidelines (what photos to add for maximum profile completeness)
```

### Prompt 2: Review Response Templates

```
Write review response templates for:

Business type: [restaurant / service / retail / healthcare / professional service]
Brand voice: [warm / professional / casual]

Templates for:
1. 5-star enthusiastic review (thank + reinforce specific comment + invite back)
2. 5-star review with no text (personalized acknowledgment)
3. 4-star review with minor concern (thank + address concern + invite resolution)
4. 3-star review with specific complaint (acknowledge + apologize + offer resolution)
5. 1-star unfair or false review (professional, not defensive — other customers read this)
6. Review from a regular/recognizable customer (personal, acknowledge the relationship)

Each template:
- Under 100 words
- Includes the reviewer's first name placeholder
- Addresses the specific content they mentioned
- Ends with a forward-looking statement
```

### Prompt 3: Local Content Strategy

```
Build a local content strategy for a [business type] in [city/area]:

Goal: [rank for local searches / build community authority / drive foot traffic]
Target keywords: ["[service] in [city]" variations]
Content channels: [website blog / Google Posts / social media / email]

Content plan:
1. LOCAL SEO PAGES (one-time, high-value):
   - "[Service] in [City]" landing page (structure + copy brief)
   - "[Neighborhood] [service]" pages for each area you serve
   - "Best [service] near [landmark]" content

2. COMMUNITY CONTENT (ongoing):
   - Local event coverage
   - "Guide to [neighborhood]" content
   - Partnerships with local businesses (cross-promotion)

3. EXPERTISE CONTENT (authority building):
   - "[Service] in [City]" FAQ
   - Seasonal local tips
   - Customer spotlights (with permission)

For each content type: title, target keyword, content brief.
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What's the most important local SEO factor?

Your Google Business Profile completeness and review quantity/quality together. A fully optimized GBP with 50+ reviews and consistent NAP (Name, Address, Phone) across the web outranks competitors for local searches more than any other single factor. Prompt 1 addresses GBP optimization; response templates (Prompt 2) help build review count by making every response feel personal.

### How do I get more Google reviews?

Ask directly after a positive experience — in person, via email, or via SMS. "If you had a great experience, a Google review would mean the world to us — it takes 30 seconds." Make it easy: include a direct review link (your GBP URL with /write-a-review appended). Never incentivize reviews (violates Google policy).

### How do I compete with big chains as a local business?

Local specificity and community relationships. Chains can't know every neighborhood regular by name or support the local Little League team. Your content should include local landmarks, neighborhoods, events, and community references that chains would never produce. Google rewards locally relevant content for local searches.

### Should I respond to every Google review?

Yes — both positive and negative. Responding to positive reviews increases the likelihood of repeat business and signals to Google that you're an active, engaged business. Responding professionally to negative reviews shows prospective customers how you handle problems. The audience for your negative review response is every future customer who reads it, not the original reviewer.

### What's the most important page on a local business website?

Your contact page (many customers go directly there) and your location page (helps with local SEO). If you serve multiple areas, create a dedicated page for each area you serve. "Plumber in Austin" and "Plumber in Round Rock" are different pages, each targeting customers in that specific area with locally relevant content.
