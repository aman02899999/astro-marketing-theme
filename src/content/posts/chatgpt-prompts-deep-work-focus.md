Focused, uninterrupted work is the scarcest resource in knowledge work. These prompts help you design systems around your attention — identifying your highest-value thinking time, building rituals that trigger deep focus, and eliminating the invisible friction that pulls you out of flow.

## Focus System Design Prompts

### Prompt 1: Deep Work Schedule Architect

```
Design a deep work schedule for:

My job: [description of what I do]
My most cognitively demanding work: [what requires your best thinking]
Work hours: [when you work]
Known energy patterns: [when you're sharpest — morning / afternoon / evening]
Non-negotiable commitments: [meetings, calls, obligations you can't move]
Deep work goal: [X hours per day / week]

Design:
1. Daily time blocks for deep work (specific hours, not "morning")
2. What types of work go in deep vs. shallow blocks
3. How to handle unexpected interruptions
4. A "meeting wall" strategy (batching meetings to protect deep blocks)
5. Email and communication schedule (process at X times per day only)
6. Environmental setup for each block type
7. Transition rituals (how to shift modes)

Assume I'm not starting from zero — help me restructure what I have.
```

### Prompt 2: Distraction Audit

```
Help me audit my distraction landscape:

My work setup: [home office / open office / hybrid / café]
Devices I use for work: [list]
Biggest focus killers I'm aware of: [list]
How I currently handle notifications: [describe]
My longest uninterrupted focus session recently: [X minutes]

Produce:
1. Distraction categories (internal vs. external) with specific examples
2. My likely highest-cost distraction (time × frequency)
3. Elimination vs. reduction vs. scheduling strategy for each
4. Phone protocol for deep work sessions
5. Digital environment setup checklist
6. One "nuclear option" tool or method to try if standard approaches fail
7. Realistic target: uninterrupted focus in [X] weeks

Be specific. "Use your phone less" is not advice.
```

### Prompt 3: Focus Ritual Designer

```
Design pre-focus and shutdown rituals for deep work:

Session length: [90 minutes / 2 hours / 3 hours]
Work environment: [home / office / café]
My biggest obstacle to starting: [what holds you back from beginning — scrolling / email / anxiety / unclear task]
My biggest obstacle to staying: [what pulls you out — notifications / discomfort / task switching]
Preferred ritual length: [5 / 10 / 15 minutes]

Design:
PRE-WORK RITUAL (before each deep work session):
1. Physical preparation (X steps, under 5 minutes)
2. Digital preparation (notifications, apps, browser)
3. Intent-setting (one sentence — what you're doing and why)
4. Optional: stimulus cue (music, scent, location if you can vary it)

SHUTDOWN RITUAL (after each session):
1. Task completion signal
2. Next-action capture
3. Physical closure action

DAILY SHUTDOWN RITUAL (end of workday):
- Clear the decks completely so tomorrow starts clean
- The "shutdown complete" phrase (Cal Newport's approach)
```

## Flow State Prompts

### Prompt 4: Flow Trigger Identifier

```
Help me identify and engineer my personal flow triggers:

My most creative/productive work looks like: [describe moments when you've been in flow]
The conditions that were present: [where, when, what you were working on, any rituals]
What typically breaks my flow: [list]
Work I find intrinsically engaging: [what pulls you in effortlessly]
Work that requires forced discipline: [what you procrastinate on]

Identify:
1. My personal flow triggers (conditions that make flow more likely)
2. My personal flow killers (conditions that guarantee shallow work)
3. The optimal task difficulty level for my flow (too easy = boredom, too hard = anxiety)
4. Environmental engineering recommendations
5. A 21-day experiment to systematically improve my flow frequency

Base this on flow research (Csikszentmihalyi's conditions: clear goals, immediate feedback, challenge-skill balance).
```

### Prompt 5: Weekly Schedule Optimizer

```
Optimize my weekly schedule for maximum deep work:

My current weekly schedule:
[describe or paste your typical week — meetings, commitments, tasks]

My role requires: [what you need to produce each week]
Deep work needed: [minimum hours for your most important work]
Shallow work I can't eliminate: [emails, admin, meetings]

Optimization principles to apply:
1. Meeting batching (group meetings to protect long blocks)
2. Energy matching (deep work during peak energy hours)
3. Theme days (dedicate entire days to one type of work)
4. Buffer blocks (protect against schedule drift)
5. The weekly plan format that works for my brain

Output: a restructured weekly template I can actually adopt starting Monday.
Identify: which 3 meetings on my schedule are most worth protecting vs. challenging.
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many hours of deep work is realistic per day?

Cal Newport's research suggests 3–4 hours of genuinely deep work per day is the sustainable ceiling for most knowledge workers. Elite performers max out around 4 hours. Trying to do 8 hours of deep work produces diminishing returns and burnout. Start with 1–2 focused hours and build from there — quality matters more than volume.

### What if my job doesn't allow for deep work blocks?

Most jobs have more flexibility than people realize. Start by identifying the one 90-minute window per day that is most protected — early morning before others arrive, during lunch, or the last 90 minutes of the day. The goal isn't a perfect system; it's reclaiming the one best window you have. Prompt 1 helps identify this.

### Does listening to music help or hurt focus?

It depends on the task and the person. For mechanical, repetitive work: music (especially lyric-free) often helps. For language-intensive deep work (writing, complex analysis): lyric-free music is neutral to slightly helpful for most people; music with lyrics is almost universally harmful. Try binaural beats or ambient soundscapes if music with lyrics hurts your concentration.

### How do I stop my phone from destroying my focus sessions?

Physical separation is the only reliable solution. Put your phone in another room during deep work blocks. "Silent mode" doesn't work — you still check it. "App blockers" require willpower to override. Physical distance doesn't. This is the single highest-ROI change most people can make to their focus, and it takes about 10 seconds to implement.

### How long does it take to improve at deep work?

The capacity for sustained concentration improves with practice like a muscle. Most people see significant improvement in 3–4 weeks of consistent daily practice. The first two weeks feel uncomfortable — your brain has been trained by years of frequent interruption and will resist sustained focus. Stick to the rituals from Prompt 3 through the discomfort; it gets significantly easier.
