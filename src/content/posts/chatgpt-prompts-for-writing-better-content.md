Writing with AI isn't about replacing your voice — it's about removing the friction between your ideas and the page. These 50 ChatGPT prompts cut straight through writer's block and give you drafts, outlines, and polished copy in minutes.

> **How to use these:** Replace every part in [brackets] with your specifics. The more detail you add, the better the output.

## Blog Writing Prompts

### Prompt 1: Complete Blog Post from a Title

```
Write a comprehensive blog post:

Title: "[your title]"
Target audience: [who will read this]
Primary keyword: "[keyword]" — use naturally throughout
Word count: [1,500–2,500]
Tone: [conversational / authoritative / casual / professional]

Structure:
- Hook opening (no "In today's world" or "In this article")
- 5–7 H2 sections with practical detail
- At least 3 real-world examples
- Actionable takeaway in each section
- Conclusion with a single clear CTA

Do not pad. Every sentence must earn its place.
```

---

### Prompt 2: Blog Post Outline Generator

```
Create a detailed blog post outline for:

Topic: "[topic]"
Target keyword: "[keyword]"
Audience: [reader type and knowledge level]
Goal: [what the reader should be able to do after reading]

Output:
- H1 title (SEO optimized)
- Meta description (155 characters)
- H2 sections with H3 sub-points for each
- Key stat or example to include in each section
- FAQ section (5 questions)
- Suggested internal links (leave as [INTERNAL LINK: topic])
```

### Prompt 3: Rewrite Weak Content

```
Rewrite this content to be significantly better:

[paste your draft]

Problems to fix:
- [problem 1, e.g. too vague]
- [problem 2, e.g. no examples]
- [problem 3, e.g. passive voice throughout]

Keep: [anything to preserve — tone, specific facts, structure]
Improve: [what needs the most work]
Target reading level: [grade 8 / professional / expert]
```

## Email Writing Prompts

### Prompt 4: Cold Outreach Email

```
Write a cold outreach email:

From: [your role and company]
To: [recipient role and company type]
Goal: [what you want them to do]
Value proposition: [what's in it for them — be specific]
Pain point you're solving: [their problem]

Requirements:
- Subject line: 6 words or fewer, no clickbait
- Opening: reference something specific about them (leave as [PERSONALIZATION])
- Body: under 100 words
- One clear CTA only
- No: "I hope this email finds you well", "synergy", "circle back"
```

---

### Prompt 5: Email Sequence (5 emails)

```
Write a 5-email nurture sequence for:

Product/service: [what you're selling]
Target customer: [who they are, what they want]
Sequence goal: [trial signup / purchase / demo booking]

Email 1: Welcome + deliver lead magnet (day 0)
Email 2: Educational value, no pitch (day 2)
Email 3: Case study or social proof (day 4)
Email 4: Objection handling (day 6)
Email 5: Offer + urgency (day 8)

Each email: subject line, preview text, body under 200 words, CTA.
```

## Landing Page and Sales Copy Prompts

### Prompt 6: Landing Page Hero Section

```
Write the hero section for a landing page:

Product: [product name and one-sentence description]
Target customer: [who they are]
Primary benefit: [the main outcome they get]
Biggest objection: [what holds them back]

Output:
- Headline (under 10 words, benefit-focused)
- Subheadline (1–2 sentences expanding the headline)
- 3 bullet points (feature → benefit format)
- Primary CTA button text (3–5 words)
- Secondary CTA (for non-buyers)
- Trust signal line (e.g. "Join 10,000+ marketers")
```

---

### Prompt 7: Full Sales Page

```
Write a long-form sales page for:

Product: [name]
Price: [price point]
Target customer: [detailed description]
Core transformation: [before state → after state]
Key features: [list 5–7]
Objections to address: [list 3]
Social proof available: [testimonials / case studies / numbers]

Structure:
1. Headline + subheadline
2. Pain section (agitate the problem)
3. Solution introduction
4. Features + benefits breakdown
5. Social proof block
6. Objection handling (FAQ style)
7. Pricing + guarantee
8. Final CTA
```

## Social and Ad Copy Prompts

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I stop ChatGPT from sounding generic?

Two things: give it a specific style reference (a real author, brand, or publication you want to sound like), and forbid the phrases it overuses. Explicitly say "Do not use: 'In today's world', 'leverage', 'game-changer', 'dive into'." You'll immediately get cleaner copy.

### Can ChatGPT write content that ranks on Google?

Yes, with the right prompts. Specify your target keyword, include it in the content brief, ask for a proper structure with H2s and H3s, and always add your own expertise, data, and examples. AI-drafted + human-enhanced content ranks well.

### What's the best prompt for writing emails that get replies?

Prompt 4 (cold outreach) works best when you fill in the personalization placeholder with something real. The constraint on length (under 100 words) is critical — most AI-written emails are too long.

### How do I make ChatGPT maintain a consistent tone?

Include a "Tone profile" in your system prompt or at the top of your message: 3–5 adjectives, a style reference author, one sentence to avoid, and one example sentence that captures the tone. Save this as a reusable prompt prefix.

### Should I always edit AI-written content?

Yes — always. Use it as a first draft, not a final product. The value is speed: go from blank page to 80% done in minutes, then spend your editing time on the last 20% where your expertise and voice make the real difference.
