Backlinks remain one of Google's most important ranking signals. But most link building fails because the outreach is generic, the value exchange is unclear, or the linkable asset isn't worth linking to. These prompts fix all three.

## Linkable Asset Creation Prompts

### Prompt 1: Linkable Asset Brief

```
Design a linkable asset for earning backlinks in the [niche] industry:

My website: [description and URL]
Target domains to earn links from: [type of sites — blogs / news / .edu / tools / resources]
My content capabilities: [data analysis / original research / interactive tools / comprehensive guides]
Budget/resources: [time available, team skills]

Design 3 linkable asset concepts:

Asset 1: [Data-driven research piece]
- What data I could analyze or survey
- Who would link to this and why
- Estimated links it could earn

Asset 2: [Comprehensive resource]
- What gap this fills
- Who maintains resource pages in this niche
- Why they'd add this vs. competitors' resources

Asset 3: [Interactive tool or calculator]
- What problem it solves
- Embed potential (who would embed this on their site)
- Link earning mechanism

Recommend: which asset has the best links/effort ratio for my site.
```

### Prompt 2: Original Research Design

```
Design an original research study for link earning:

My industry: [niche]
Research goal: [what question you want to answer with data]
Data I can access: [surveys / existing datasets / customer data / public data]
Target publications to cover this research: [types of outlets]

Research design:
1. Headline finding (the one surprising stat that will get coverage)
2. Methodology (how you'll collect/analyze data — simple enough to execute)
3. Survey questions if applicable (10 questions that yield quotable stats)
4. How to present findings (report / infographic / interactive)
5. Outreach targets (who covers this type of research)
6. Timing for maximum relevance (seasonal / news-cycle aware)
7. Press hook (why this is newsworthy NOW)

Example strong data headlines for my industry: [generate 5]
```

## Outreach Prompts

### Prompt 3: Resource Page Link Request

```
Write an outreach email for resource page link building:

My content: [title and URL — what you want linked]
Target page: [describe the resource page — its topic, quality, owner's niche]
Connection: [any mutual connection / how you found them / why you're right for their audience]

Email requirements:
- Subject line: reference their page specifically (not generic)
- Compliment their resource page (genuine — reference a specific resource on it)
- Make the case why my content belongs there
- Under 100 words
- No "please" at the start
- Clear, specific ask

Write 3 variations:
1. Direct/brief approach
2. Value-first approach (offer something before asking)
3. Personalized approach (reference their recent content)
```

### Prompt 4: Broken Link Building Outreach

```
Write a broken link building outreach email:

Situation: I found a broken link on [their page topic] pointing to a now-dead resource about [topic]
My replacement: [your content that covers the same topic]
Their site type: [blog / resource page / industry site]

Email requirements:
- Lead with the helpful find (you're doing them a favor)
- Be specific about which link is broken (they appreciate precision)
- Position my replacement naturally — not as a pitch, as a service
- Under 80 words
- Friendly, not transactional tone

Also write: the follow-up email if they don't respond in 7 days (under 40 words).
```

### Prompt 5: HARO/Source Response

```
Write a response to this HARO/Connectively journalist query:

Query: [paste the journalist's question]
Publication: [outlet name]
Journalist: [their focus]
My expertise: [what qualifies you to answer this]

Response format:
- Credentials line (one sentence — most relevant credential only)
- Answer: direct, quotable, 100–150 words
- One specific stat, example, or insight that adds unique value
- Bio line (for attribution)
- Contact details

Rules:
- Answer the question asked (don't pitch your company)
- Be quotable — write in complete sentences, no jargon
- Deliver the insight in the first sentence
- No "great question!" or similar filler
```

## Strategy Prompts

### Prompt 6: Link Building Campaign Plan

```
Build a 90-day link building campaign for:

Website: [URL and niche]
Current domain authority/rating: [X]
Target: [earn X links in 90 days]
Content assets I have: [list linkable content]
Team: [just me / small team]

Campaign structure:

Month 1: Foundation
- Linkable asset audit (what do I have worth linking to?)
- Quick win opportunities (resource pages, directories, partner sites)
- Target list building (X prospects per week)

Month 2: Outreach at Scale
- Primary outreach method (based on assets and niche)
- Daily prospecting and email volume
- Follow-up cadence

Month 3: Digital PR
- Research asset launch
- Journalist outreach
- Syndication targets

Weekly metrics to track: [outreach sent / responses / links earned / conversion rate]
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many backlinks do I need to rank?

It depends entirely on your competition. Use a tool like Ahrefs or Semrush to check the backlink profiles of the top 3 pages ranking for your target keyword. Match or exceed their domain authority and link count over time. "How many" is less important than "how authoritative" — 5 links from highly trusted sites outrank 100 links from low-quality sites.

### Is cold outreach for links still effective?

Yes, with very targeted, very personalized outreach. Mass generic outreach gets 1–3% response rates. Highly targeted outreach (genuine value, specific personalization, relevant content) gets 10–25% response rates. The quality of your linkable asset matters most — outreach is just distribution for content worth linking to.

### Should I buy backlinks?

No — paid links violate Google's guidelines and risk a manual penalty that can tank rankings. Google is increasingly effective at detecting paid link schemes. The tactics in this guide (earned links through content and genuine outreach) are slower but build lasting authority that compounds. Paid links often evaporate when the scheme is detected, taking your rankings with them.

### What's the best type of backlink?

Links from topically relevant sites with genuine authority — a real site in your niche that humans actually visit. Domain authority/rating matters less than relevance and trust. A link from a small but highly respected industry publication often outperforms a link from a high-DA generic content farm. Editorial links (someone chose to link to you, unprompted) are the most valuable.

### How do I find sites to get links from?

Start with: (1) your competitors' backlink profiles (in Ahrefs/Semrush — find who links to them but not you), (2) resource pages in your niche (search: "[your topic] + resources" or "[your topic] + useful links"), (3) industry publications that cover your topic, (4) journalists who cover your industry on HARO/Connectively. Prompt 6's campaign plan structures these into a systematic approach.
