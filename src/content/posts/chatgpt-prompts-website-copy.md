Your website copy is your best salesperson — working 24/7, never having an off day, and reaching customers you'd never meet in person. These prompts help you write every page of your site with the clarity and persuasion that turns browsers into buyers.

## Core Page Prompts

### Prompt 1: Homepage Hero Copy

```
Write homepage hero copy for:

Business: [name and what you do]
Target customer: [specific description]
Primary outcome you deliver: [the transformation or result]
Differentiator: [what makes you different from alternatives]
Social proof: [one credibility signal — customers / years / awards]

Hero section (above the fold):
H1 HEADLINE: [benefit-focused, under 10 words, customer as subject]
SUBHEADLINE: [1–2 sentences expanding the promise with specificity]
CTA BUTTON: [3–5 words — action verb + benefit]
TRUST SIGNAL: [below CTA — "Trusted by 10,000+ customers" or equivalent]

Write 3 headline variations:
1. Outcome-focused ("Get [result] without [pain]")
2. Customer-as-hero ("Finally, [description of their better situation]")
3. Unique mechanism ("The [your approach] that [outcome]")
```

### Prompt 2: About Page

```
Write an about page for:

Business: [name]
Founder(s): [brief background relevant to the business]
Origin story: [what led to starting this — be honest, not generic]
What you believe: [your core conviction about your industry]
Who you serve: [your customers and why you care about them]
What makes you different: [a genuine differentiator]

Structure:
1. Opening hook (why this business exists — a specific problem or moment)
2. Our belief/mission (what we stand for — not a mission statement, a genuine belief)
3. How we work (what's different about your approach)
4. The team / founder story (relevant background, not a CV)
5. Who we serve (describe your ideal customer and acknowledge them)
6. CTA: [what you want them to do next]

Tone: human, specific, conviction-driven. No corporate platitudes.
```

### Prompt 3: Service or Product Page

```
Write a service page for: [service name]

Business: [description]
Service: [what it includes]
Target customer: [who this service is for]
Primary outcome: [what they get from this service]
Price: [$X — or "contact for pricing"]
Ideal for vs. not for: [specific fit criteria]

Page structure:
1. H1: service name + primary outcome
2. Opening paragraph: who this is for and what changes
3. What's included (format: clear deliverables, not vague descriptions)
4. How it works (3–5 steps — make the process feel simple)
5. Who this is perfect for (3–4 bullet personas)
6. Results or proof (metrics, testimonials, case study reference)
7. Investment section (pricing + what it includes)
8. FAQ (4–6 questions specific to this service)
9. CTA (clear next step)
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long should website copy be?

Long enough to persuade a skeptical visitor; short enough that they don't leave before they reach the CTA. Homepage: 400–600 words. Service/product pages: 600–1,200 words. Landing pages: varies dramatically by traffic temperature — cold traffic needs more persuasion (1,000–2,500 words); warm retargeting traffic needs less.

### Should I write for SEO or for conversion first?

Conversion first, then SEO optimization. Copy that converts poorly but ranks doesn't grow your business. Copy that's clear and persuasive often ranks well because users stay on the page (positive engagement signals). Add keyword integration as a final step, not a first draft constraint.

### What's the most common website copy mistake?

Talking about yourself instead of your customer. Every headline that says "We are..." or "Our company..." misses an opportunity to say "You get..." or "Your business will...". The customer is the hero of every page on your site. Your business is the guide that helps them.

### How do I write an about page that doesn't feel like a resume?

Lead with belief, not biography. "We believe that [industry problem] doesn't have to be this way" is more compelling than "Founded in 2019, [Company] has grown to serve..." Tell the story of why you started, what you believe is wrong with the industry, and how you're different. Reserve the credentials for a brief closing credibility section.

### How do I write a CTA that gets clicked?

Make it specific to what happens next. "Get Started" is vague. "Book Your Free 30-Minute Strategy Call" tells them exactly what to expect. Specificity reduces anxiety ("what am I signing up for?") and increases clicks. Add a benefit where possible: "Get My Free Audit" beats "Submit Form."
