Nonprofit staff do more with less than almost any other sector. These prompts help you write grant proposals, donor appeals, and impact stories faster — without sacrificing the authenticity that makes nonprofit communication effective.

## Grant Writing Prompts

### Prompt 1: Grant Proposal Sections

```
Write grant proposal sections for:

Organization: [name and mission]
Grant: [foundation name and focus area]
Project: [name and description]
Funding requested: [$X]
Project duration: [X months]
Target population served: [who benefits and how many]
Problem statement data: [statistics or evidence you have]
Our track record: [relevant past work]

Write these sections:
1. Executive summary (250 words — stands alone, tells the whole story)
2. Problem statement (400 words — evidence-based, localized to your community)
3. Project description (500 words — what you'll do, how, and why it works)
4. Evaluation plan (200 words — how you'll measure success)
5. Organizational capacity (200 words — why you're qualified to execute this)

Follow: the foundation's stated priorities and language from their guidelines.
Insert [DATA NEEDED] where I need to add specific numbers.
```

### Prompt 2: Donor Appeal Letter

```
Write a donor appeal letter for:

Organization: [name and mission]
Campaign: [what you're raising for — specific project or general operations]
Goal: [$X by date]
Audience: [lapsed donors / major donors / first-time givers / annual fund]
Season: [year-end / spring / emergency appeal]
Impact story to feature: [describe a beneficiary story — real or hypothetical]
Specific ask: [$X gift amounts to suggest — e.g. $50 / $100 / $250]

Letter structure:
1. Opening: a story (not a statistic) — the reader's first 3 seconds
2. The problem (brief — don't dwell)
3. Your solution and what it achieves
4. The beneficiary story in full
5. The ask (specific — "your gift of $100 will...")
6. The deadline and why it matters
7. Thank you and re-statement of impact
8. P.S. (the second-most-read element — make it count)

Tone: warm, personal, urgent — not corporate.
```

## Impact Communication Prompts

### Prompt 3: Annual Impact Report Copy

```
Write sections of our annual impact report:

Organization: [name and mission]
Year: [year]
Key data:
  - People served: [X]
  - Programs: [list]
  - Financial summary: [revenue / program expenses / ratio]
  - Volunteer hours: [X]
  - Key outcomes: [list measurable results]
Story of the year: [one person or community story]

Write:
1. Executive director letter (400 words — personal, honest, forward-looking)
2. Program highlights (3 programs, 150 words each)
3. Impact statistics page copy (how to frame the numbers as stories)
4. Community voice section (quotes format for beneficiary testimonials)
5. Thank you section (donors/volunteers/partners, 200 words)
```

### Prompt 4: Volunteer Recruitment and Engagement

```
Create volunteer recruitment and engagement content for:

Organization: [name]
Volunteer roles needed: [list roles with time commitments]
Skills required: [describe what you're looking for]
Target volunteer: [demographic and motivation — retirees / professionals / students]

Generate:
1. Volunteer recruitment flyer copy (headlines + body + CTA)
2. Social media post (3 versions for different platforms)
3. Volunteer welcome email (first week onboarding)
4. Monthly volunteer newsletter template (recognize / update / ask)
5. Thank you letter for long-term volunteers
6. Exit survey (5 questions to understand why volunteers leave)
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I make ChatGPT grant proposals competitive?

Add your organization's unique data, community context, and specific evidence at every opportunity. Grant reviewers read dozens of proposals — specificity wins. "Serving 150 low-income youth in a ZIP code with 42% poverty rate" beats "serving youth in need." ChatGPT gives you the structure; you fill it with the evidence that makes your proposal stand out.

### Can ChatGPT write a grant proposal from scratch?

It can produce a complete first draft if you provide the key information (Prompt 1). But grants require your organization's specific data, outcomes evidence, and alignment with the funder's priorities. Use ChatGPT to write the structure and polish the prose; you provide the substance that makes it fundable.

### What's the most important section of a donor appeal letter?

The opening story. Donors make emotional decisions first and rational decisions second. A specific, human story in the first paragraph determines whether the rest gets read. Prompt 2 starts with a beneficiary story for exactly this reason. Data, impact, and the ask work once the emotional connection is established.

### How do I use ChatGPT to improve donor retention?

Write personalized thank-you communications (not just receipts), impact updates that show donors what their specific gift accomplished, and cultivation content that keeps your mission front of mind between asks. ChatGPT makes it possible to segment and personalize communications that would take days to write manually.

### Should nonprofits disclose using AI in their communications?

There's no legal requirement currently, but transparency in donor-facing communications aligns with nonprofit values. Most organizations treat AI as a drafting and efficiency tool — the message, mission, and data remain genuine. If a donor asks, being honest about using AI tools to improve efficiency is appropriate and increasingly expected.
