HR professionals produce more documentation than almost any other function. These prompts reduce the time spent on routine documents without reducing the care and specificity that makes HR communication effective.

## Performance Review Prompts

### Prompt 1: Performance Review Writer

```
Write a performance review for:

Employee: [role and tenure]
Review period: [timeframe]
Performance level: [exceeds / meets / partially meets / does not meet expectations]

Accomplishments I want to highlight:
[Paste your notes — rough is fine]

Areas for development:
[List development areas]

Goals for next period:
[List 2–3 goals]

Write a professional performance review with:
1. Overall performance summary (2 paragraphs — specific, evidence-based)
2. Accomplishments section (5–7 bullets with specific examples)
3. Development areas section (constructive, growth-focused framing)
4. Goals for next period (SMART format)
5. Closing statement (forward-looking, supportive)

Tone: professional, specific, fair. No vague platitudes ("great team player").
```

### Prompt 2: Job Description Generator

```
Write a job description for:

Role: [title]
Company: [description]
Team: [what team this reports to]
Responsibilities: [list key duties]
Required qualifications: [must-have skills and experience]
Preferred qualifications: [nice-to-have]
Compensation: [$X–$Y or "competitive"]
Location: [remote / hybrid / on-site, city]
Culture notes: [what makes this company a good place to work]

Job description sections:
1. About the company (3 sentences — mission, culture, why join)
2. The role (what they'll do and why it matters)
3. Key responsibilities (5–7 bullets — specific, active verbs)
4. What you'll bring (required qualifications — honest, not inflated)
5. Nice to have (preferred but not required)
6. What we offer (compensation + benefits + culture)
7. About our team / hiring process (optional but builds trust)

Apply inclusive language guidelines: no gender-coded words, no unnecessary experience inflation.
```

### Prompt 3: Difficult Employee Conversation Script

```
Help me prepare for this employee conversation:

Situation: [describe the performance/behavioral issue]
Employee context: [tenure, role, past performance]
Goal: [improvement / performance improvement plan / separation]
My concern: [what I'm worried about going wrong]

Script for the conversation:
1. Opening (purpose and tone — serious but supportive)
2. Stating the issue (specific, factual, non-personal)
3. Getting their perspective (active listening questions)
4. Stating the expectation clearly
5. Agreeing on next steps and timeline
6. Documentation language (what to write in the follow-up email)

Also: what NOT to say in this conversation (3 phrases that could create legal risk or escalate emotion).
Note: consult with legal counsel for termination or PIP conversations.
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What makes a good performance review?

Specificity. "Great communication skills" helps no one. "Presented the Q3 roadmap to 50 stakeholders and received strong positive feedback" is memorable and actionable. Performance reviews should contain specific examples, not general impressions. ChatGPT's review is only as good as the specific examples you provide in the prompt.

### How do I frame development areas constructively?

Frame as growth opportunities, not failures. "Continuing to develop executive presence in cross-functional meetings" vs. "is not confident in meetings." Tie development areas to specific future goals: "As [employee] takes on more senior responsibilities, developing [skill] will be important for [specific upcoming opportunity]."

### What language should I avoid in job descriptions?

Gender-coded language that deters qualified candidates: "rockstar," "ninja," "aggressive" (skews male). Unnecessarily inflated requirements: "10+ years experience" for a role that reasonably requires 3–5. Requirements that screen out protected groups without business justification. Focus on what the role actually requires.

### How do I document a performance conversation appropriately?

Send a follow-up email within 24 hours that summarizes: the issue discussed, the expectations stated, the improvement plan agreed, and the timeline. Don't editorialize — factual documentation of what was said. This creates a record that protects both the employee and the company if the situation escalates.

### When should HR involve legal counsel?

Before any performance improvement plan, before any termination (especially involuntary), for any situation involving protected characteristics (race, age, gender, disability, pregnancy, religion), and for any severance agreement. The cost of an employment attorney consultation is always less than the cost of an employment lawsuit.
