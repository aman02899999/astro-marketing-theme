Marketing teams using AI aren't just saving time — they're running more campaigns, testing more ideas, and producing more content than their competitors. These 100 prompts cover every marketing function, all copy-paste ready.

## Content Strategy Prompts

### Prompt 1: Content Strategy Builder

```
Build a 90-day content strategy for:

Brand: [company name and what you do]
Target audience: [detailed customer description]
Business goal: [what the content must support — leads, sales, awareness]
Channels: [blog / LinkedIn / Instagram / YouTube / email — pick yours]
Resources: [team size, posting frequency you can sustain]

Deliver:
1. Content pillars (3–5 themes that align brand + audience)
2. Content mix (educational / inspirational / promotional ratio)
3. Monthly content calendar template
4. 20 specific content ideas per pillar
5. KPIs to track for each channel
```

---

### Prompt 2: Content Repurposing Engine

```
I have this piece of content:
[paste your blog post / video transcript / podcast notes]

Repurpose it into all of the following:
1. LinkedIn post (hook + 5 bullet points + CTA)
2. Twitter/X thread (8–10 tweets)
3. Instagram carousel (5 slides with captions)
4. Email newsletter section (200 words)
5. YouTube video script outline
6. 3 short-form video hooks (for Reels/TikTok/Shorts)
7. Quote graphic text (1 punchy sentence)
8. FAQ addition for our website

Keep the core message consistent across all formats.
```

## Brand Voice Prompts

### Prompt 3: Brand Voice Guide Generator

```
Create a brand voice guide for:

Company: [name]
What we do: [one sentence]
Our customers: [who they are]
Our personality (pick 3–5): [bold / warm / witty / authoritative / playful / direct / etc.]
Brands we admire for their voice: [2–3 examples]
Brands we do NOT want to sound like: [1–2 examples]

Produce a brand voice guide with:
1. Voice personality description (1 paragraph)
2. 4 voice traits with "We are / We are not" examples
3. Vocabulary: words we use vs. words we avoid
4. Tone variations (how we adjust for: sales pages / social media / customer support / crisis)
5. 3 before/after rewrites showing the voice in action
```

## Email Marketing Prompts

### Prompt 4: Welcome Email Sequence

```
Write a 5-email welcome sequence for new subscribers to:

Brand: [name]
Lead magnet they downloaded: [what they got]
Audience: [who subscribes]
Sequence goal: [move them toward: first purchase / demo booking / engagement]

Email 1: Deliver lead magnet + set expectations (day 0)
Email 2: Your brand story — why you exist (day 2)
Email 3: Best resource or most popular content (day 4)
Email 4: Social proof — customer story (day 6)
Email 5: Soft offer or invitation (day 9)

Each email: subject line, preview text, body (150–200 words), CTA.
```

---

### Prompt 5: Promotional Campaign Emails

```
Write a 3-email promotional campaign for:

Offer: [what you're promoting]
Discount or incentive: [if any]
Urgency/deadline: [end date or limited quantity]
Audience: [your subscriber list description]

Email 1 (Launch): Announce the offer with full details
Email 2 (Mid): Reminder with a new angle — testimonial, FAQ, or how-to
Email 3 (Close): Final hours urgency email

Each email: subject line (5 variations), preview text, body, CTA button text.
```

## Advertising and Paid Media Prompts

### Prompt 6: Google Ads Headlines and Descriptions

```
Write Google Ads copy for:

Product/service: [description]
Target keyword: "[keyword]"
Landing page offer: [what they get when they click]
Main benefit: [primary outcome]
Differentiator: [what sets you apart]

Produce:
- 15 headlines (max 30 characters each, include keyword in at least 5)
- 4 descriptions (max 90 characters each)
- 2 callout extensions
- 3 sitelink titles with descriptions

Label which headlines are: Keyword-focused / Benefit-focused / CTA-focused
```

### Prompt 7: Campaign Brief Generator

```
Write a complete marketing campaign brief for:

Campaign name: [name]
Goal: [specific, measurable objective]
Audience: [detailed targeting description]
Budget: [$X total]
Timeline: [start and end dates]
Channels: [list channels]

Include:
1. Campaign overview (2 paragraphs)
2. Target audience persona
3. Key messages per channel
4. Success metrics and targets
5. Creative direction notes
6. Approval and launch checklist
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many marketing prompts should I use at once?

Start with the ones that match your current biggest bottleneck. If you spend most time on content creation, start with Prompts 1–3. If email is your focus, start with 4–5. Master 5 prompts before adding more.

### Can ChatGPT replace a marketing team?

No — it replaces repetitive tasks within a marketing team. Strategy, customer understanding, creative direction, and relationship building still require humans. AI handles the production layer.

### How do I maintain brand consistency across AI-generated content?

Create a brand voice guide using Prompt 3, then paste it at the top of every subsequent prompt. Tell ChatGPT: "Always follow this brand voice guide:" and paste the guide. Consistent input = consistent output.

### What marketing tasks does ChatGPT do best?

First-draft content, email sequences, ad copy variations, repurposing existing content, campaign briefs, and brainstorming. It's weakest on nuanced brand strategy, competitor intelligence, and anything requiring real-time data.

### Should I disclose that content was AI-assisted?

Requirements vary by platform and jurisdiction. For editorial content, many publishers require disclosure. For marketing copy, there's generally no legal requirement — but authenticity and accuracy matter more than the tool you used.
