Writing is thinking. The act of articulating an idea clearly reveals whether you actually understand it. These prompts use ChatGPT as a thinking partner — to structure arguments, stress-test ideas, improve clarity, and overcome the friction that stops you from writing.

## Thinking Clarity Prompts

### Prompt 1: Idea Clarifier

```
Help me clarify this idea:

My rough idea: [describe it as you currently understand it — messy is fine]
Context: [why you're thinking about this]
What I'm trying to figure out: [the underlying question]

Help me:
1. Restate my idea more precisely (in 2 sentences)
2. Identify the core claim I'm making
3. Separate what I know from what I'm assuming
4. Find the one thing I still need to figure out
5. Ask me the one question that would clarify this most

Don't answer for me — help me think better.
```

---

### Prompt 2: Argument Structurer

```
Help me structure this argument:

My position: [what you believe]
Evidence I have: [what supports your position]
Context: [for what purpose — essay / presentation / email / conversation]
Audience: [who I'm persuading]
Their likely objection: [what the other side would say]

Structure my argument using:
1. Claim (my position stated precisely)
2. Warrant (why the evidence supports the claim)
3. Evidence (ordered from strongest to supporting)
4. Acknowledgment of the strongest counterargument
5. Rebuttal (why my position holds despite the counterargument)
6. Conclusion (what follows if I'm right)

Show me: where my argument is weakest and what would strengthen it.
```

### Prompt 3: Mental Model Application

```
Apply [mental model name] to this problem:

Mental model: [e.g. First Principles / Inversion / Second-Order Thinking / Occam's Razor / Regret Minimization]
My problem: [describe the situation or decision]

Walk me through:
1. How this mental model applies to my situation
2. What questions it asks me to answer
3. What my situation looks like through this lens
4. What insights this reveals that I might have missed
5. What action this suggests

Also: which other mental model would give a useful contrasting perspective?
```

## Writing Improvement Prompts

### Prompt 4: Writing Coach

```
Edit this writing and make it significantly better:

[paste your draft — any length]

Purpose: [what this piece is for — email / report / article / pitch]
Audience: [who reads this]
Main weakness I suspect: [vague / too long / unclear structure / passive voice / etc.]

Improve:
1. Opening — does it earn the read? If not, rewrite it
2. Structure — is the argument logical and clear?
3. Sentence level — cut 20% of the words without losing meaning
4. Replace every passive construction with active
5. Replace abstract words with concrete ones
6. Cut any sentence that doesn't advance the main point
7. Ending — does it land? Rewrite if not

Show before/after for the most improved sections. Explain the key changes.
```

---

### Prompt 5: Explain It Simply

```
Explain [complex topic] simply enough that [specific audience] would understand it:

Topic: [subject]
Current explanation I have: [paste or describe]
Audience: [who needs to understand this — their background, what they know]
Purpose: [why they need to understand it — decision to make / context for a meeting / etc.]

Constraints:
- No jargon (define any technical term you must use)
- Use analogies to things this audience already knows
- Maximum 200 words
- One concept at a time
- If they ask "so what?" — the answer must be obvious

After writing the explanation, tell me: what's the one thing most people still misunderstand about this topic even after a good explanation?
```

---

### Prompt 6: Writer's Block Breaker

```
I'm stuck on: [describe what you're writing and where you're stuck]

My draft so far:
[paste what you have — even if it's just a title or outline]

What I've tried: [what you've already attempted]
What's blocking me: [is it unclear direction / fear of the wrong approach / perfectionism / missing information?]

Help me unstick with:
1. Three possible directions I could take from here (meaningfully different)
2. The single next sentence I could write (just one — to break the inertia)
3. A reframe: what would this piece be if I wrote it for [different audience / different format / different purpose]?
4. The question I'm avoiding asking that would unlock this piece

Rule: don't write the piece for me. Give me the key that opens the door.
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is using ChatGPT to improve my writing cheating?

No more than using a dictionary, grammar checker, or writing coach. ChatGPT is a thinking partner and editor — the ideas, arguments, and voice remain yours. The key is using it to improve your thinking and writing, not to replace it. Prompt 4 improves your draft; Prompt 6 helps you write it yourself. These develop your writing; they don't substitute for it.

### What's the most common writing mistake professionals make?

Burying the lead. Most professional writers start with context, background, and caveats before stating the main point. Readers leave before they get there. Start every piece — email, report, pitch — with the main point in sentence one. Everything else is support. Prompt 4 specifically audits your opening.

### How do I write more clearly in my second language?

Use Prompt 5's "explain it simply" approach as a drafting strategy — write your first draft aiming for clarity over sophistication. Then ask ChatGPT to improve naturalness: "Rewrite this to sound like a fluent native speaker, preserving my exact meaning." The combination of simple drafting + AI naturalizing produces clearer, more authentic writing than trying to write perfectly the first time.

### How do I develop my own writing voice?

Write a lot, read a lot, and study writers whose voice you admire. Use Prompt 4 to get feedback, but resist AI rewrites that erase your particular way of expressing things. Your voice comes from your experiences, values, and how you naturally connect ideas — ChatGPT shouldn't flatten that. Tell it "preserve my voice and fix only the structural and clarity issues."

### What's the Feynman test for writing?

Named after physicist Richard Feynman: if you can't explain an idea simply, you don't fully understand it. Use Prompt 5 regularly — not just for your audience, but as a self-test of your own understanding. Every time ChatGPT struggles to simplify your explanation, it means there's a gap in your understanding, not just your writing.
