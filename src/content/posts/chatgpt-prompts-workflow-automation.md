The highest-leverage use of AI isn't generating content — it's eliminating the repetitive cognitive work that eats your week. These prompts target the specific tasks knowledge workers do over and over: meeting notes, reports, SOPs, email management, and decision-making. Automate these once and you save hours every week, indefinitely.

## Meeting and Communication Prompts

### Prompt 1: Meeting Notes to Action Items

```
Transform these raw meeting notes into structured outputs:

[paste your notes, transcript, or voice memo transcription]

Meeting type: [standup / planning / retrospective / client call / all-hands]
Duration: [X minutes]
Attendees: [roles only]

Output:
1. Meeting summary (3–5 bullets, what was discussed)
2. Decisions made (explicitly numbered)
3. Action items (owner role + task + deadline — format: "[@role] will [task] by [date]")
4. Open questions (unresolved items needing follow-up)
5. Next meeting agenda seed (based on open items)

Format for immediate copy-paste into Slack, Notion, or email.
```

### Prompt 2: Weekly Report Generator

```
Generate a weekly status report from these inputs:

Week of: [dates]
My role: [title]
Team: [team description]

This week I:
[bullet your raw accomplishments — rough notes are fine]

Next week I plan to:
[rough notes]

Blockers:
[any issues]

Metrics (if applicable):
[paste numbers]

Transform this into a polished weekly report:
- Executive summary (2 sentences)
- Accomplishments (formatted, with impact where possible)
- Next week priorities (3–5 items with context)
- Blockers and asks (action required from others)
- Key metrics (formatted table if data provided)

Tone: [professional and concise]
Length: [1 page max]
```

### Prompt 3: Email Triage and Draft System

```
I'm processing my inbox. For each email I paste, give me:

1. CATEGORY: [Action Required / FYI / Delegate / Delete / Archive]
2. URGENCY: [Respond today / This week / No deadline]
3. RESPONSE DRAFT: [If action required — write a draft response]
4. TIME ESTIMATE: [X minutes to handle]

My role context: [your title and what you do]
My communication style: [direct / formal / casual]

First email:
[paste email]
```

## SOP and Documentation Prompts

### Prompt 4: Standard Operating Procedure Writer

```
Write a Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for:

Process name: [name]
Purpose: [why this process exists]
Who performs it: [role]
Frequency: [daily / weekly / ad-hoc]
Tools required: [list tools, systems, logins needed]

Steps I perform (rough notes — clean these up):
[paste your rough steps]

Output:
1. Process overview (2 sentences)
2. Prerequisites and setup
3. Step-by-step instructions (numbered, imperative verbs, screenshots noted as [SCREENSHOT: what to show])
4. Decision points (if X then Y)
5. Common errors and how to fix them
6. Completion check (how to know you're done)
7. Escalation path (who to contact if stuck)

Format: Ready to add to a company wiki or Notion.
```

---

### Prompt 5: Process Documentation from Brain Dump

```
I'm going to explain a process I do from memory. Turn it into clean documentation.

My brain dump:
[talk through the process in whatever order it comes to you — messy is fine]

My role: [title]
Who else is involved: [other roles]
Tools used: [list]

Output:
1. Cleaned, logical step sequence
2. Identify any gaps I left out (ask me about them)
3. Decision tree for the branching parts
4. Time estimate per step
5. Checklist version (for repeated use)
```

## Decision-Making Prompts

### Prompt 6: Decision Framework Builder

```
Help me make a decision:

Decision to make: [describe the decision clearly]
Options I'm considering:
1. [Option A]
2. [Option B]
3. [Option C, or "open to other options"]

What matters most to me: [list your criteria — cost / time / risk / reversibility / etc.]
Constraints: [hard limits — budget, timeline, technical, political]
My current instinct: [what you're leaning toward and why]

Analyze this decision:
1. Evaluate each option against my criteria (scoring matrix)
2. Identify what I might be missing
3. Flag the key risk in my preferred option
4. Recommend a decision with reasoning
5. Suggest a "test before commit" approach if applicable
```

---

### Prompt 7: Second Opinion Bot

```
I'm about to [describe decision or action].

Steelman the case AGAINST this decision:
- What could go wrong that I'm not seeing?
- What assumptions am I making that might be wrong?
- What would a skeptic say about this plan?
- What data or information should I gather before proceeding?
- Is there a cheaper, faster, or lower-risk alternative I haven't considered?

Don't hold back. I'd rather hear hard questions from you than regret later.
```

## Reporting and Analysis Prompts

### Prompt 8: Data Narrative Generator

```
Write a narrative analysis for this data:

[paste your data — table, numbers, or metrics]

Context:
- What this data measures: [explain]
- Time period: [dates]
- Target/goal: [what success looks like]
- Audience: [who will read this analysis]

Produce:
1. Executive summary (3 sentences — what happened and what it means)
2. Key findings (3–5 bullets with specific numbers)
3. Notable trends or anomalies to flag
4. Likely causes (hypotheses, not confirmed facts — label as such)
5. Recommended actions based on the data (3 specific next steps)
6. What to watch next period

Tone: [analytical and direct, no fluff]
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which workflow automation delivers the fastest ROI?

Meeting notes to action items (Prompt 1). If you attend 3 meetings per day and spend 15 minutes per meeting writing up notes, that's 45 minutes per day — or nearly 4 hours per week. Paste your raw notes into ChatGPT and get structured output in 30 seconds. This alone pays for a ChatGPT subscription many times over.

### How do I build a personal AI workflow system?

Start by auditing your week: what do you do repetitively that involves writing, organizing, or processing information? List those tasks. Build one prompt for each. Save them to a prompt library (Notion, Obsidian, or a simple text file). Run through the library once per week and you'll continuously find new time savings.

### Can I connect ChatGPT directly to my tools (Slack, Notion, Gmail)?

Via integrations and APIs, yes. Zapier, Make.com, and n8n all offer ChatGPT integrations. You can build workflows like "When a meeting ends in Google Calendar, send transcript to ChatGPT, post structured notes to Slack." These no-code automations require setup time upfront but save hours long-term.

### How do I get my team to adopt AI productivity tools?

Start with a shared prompt library. When you find a prompt that saves significant time, add it to a team Notion page with a description and example. Make adoption frictionless — one click to copy, paste, and run. The first person who saves 5 hours in one week sells the rest of the team.

### What are the limits of AI for workflow automation?

AI handles: writing, formatting, structuring, summarizing, drafting. It doesn't handle: real-time data retrieval, API calls (without plugins), anything requiring current information past its training cutoff, and tasks that require genuine human judgment about relationships, politics, or context it doesn't have. Use it confidently for the first list; cautiously or not at all for the second.
