The difference between a business that scales and one that stalls is systems. These prompts help you document, improve, and delegate the processes that keep your business running — so you can spend your time building, not firefighting.

## SOP Development Prompts

### Prompt 1: SOP Creation from Interview

```
I'm going to describe a business process. Transform it into a professional SOP.

Process: [describe the process in whatever order it comes to you — messy is fine]
Who does this: [role]
How often: [frequency]
Tools used: [software, platforms, physical tools]
Handoffs: [who passes work to or receives work from]
Quality check: [how you know it's done correctly]

Transform my description into:
1. Process title and purpose (one sentence)
2. Scope (what's included and not)
3. Prerequisites (what must be true before starting)
4. Step-by-step instructions (numbered, imperative verbs)
5. Decision points with branching logic
6. Quality checkpoints
7. Common errors and how to recover
8. Completion criteria
9. Related SOPs to link

Format: ready for a company wiki or Notion.
```

### Prompt 2: Process Improvement Analyzer

```
Analyze this business process for improvement opportunities:

Process: [describe or paste your current SOP]
Current performance: [time it takes / error rate / cost / complaints]
Desired performance: [what "good" would look like]
Constraints: [technology / budget / team skill]

Analyze using:
1. Waste identification (DOWNTIME: Defects / Overproduction / Waiting / Non-utilized talent / Transportation / Inventory / Motion / Extra processing)
2. Bottleneck analysis (where does work pile up?)
3. Automation opportunities (what could be done by software?)
4. Delegation opportunities (what requires less skill than the current owner has?)
5. Elimination opportunities (what steps add no value?)

Output: prioritized improvement recommendations with estimated time/cost savings.
```

### Prompt 3: Delegation Framework

```
Help me delegate this task effectively:

Task to delegate: [describe the task]
To: [role or person — general description]
My concern about delegating: [what worries you — quality / judgment / relationships]
Current time this takes me: [X hours per week/month]
Complexity: [simple/routine / requires judgment / high stakes]

Build a delegation plan:
1. Skill gap assessment (what does the person need to know to do this?)
2. Training approach (how to transfer the knowledge)
3. Decision boundaries (what they can decide vs. what needs escalation)
4. Success metrics (how to know it's being done well)
5. Check-in schedule (how to verify quality without micromanaging)
6. Transition timeline (from you doing it → them doing it independently)

Also: the SOP for this task (so they have written instructions).
```

## Vendor Management Prompts

### Prompt 4: Vendor Evaluation Scorecard

```
Create a vendor evaluation scorecard for selecting a [vendor type]:

What we need: [services or products required]
Key selection criteria:
  - [Criterion 1 — e.g. price]
  - [Criterion 2 — e.g. quality]
  - [Criterion 3 — e.g. reliability]
  - [Criterion 4 — e.g. support]
  - [Criterion 5 — e.g. integration]
Budget range: [$X–$Y]
Contract length: [month-to-month / annual / multi-year]
Vendors I'm evaluating: [list 3–5]

Scorecard:
- Weight each criterion (totals 100%)
- 1–5 rating scale with descriptions for each score
- Total weighted score calculation
- Minimum threshold to advance (X points)
- Red flag criteria (disqualifying regardless of score)

Include: the 5 questions I should ask each vendor in the evaluation.
```

### Prompt 5: Operational Dashboard Design

```
Design an operational dashboard for:

Business type: [description]
My role: [CEO / COO / department head]
Decision frequency: [daily / weekly / monthly]

What I need to track:
[list your key business processes and what matters in each]

Dashboard design:
1. Daily metrics (what I check every morning — max 5 numbers)
2. Weekly metrics (reviewed in weekly ops meeting — max 10)
3. Monthly metrics (strategic view — max 15)
4. Alert thresholds (when should a metric trigger action?)
5. Who owns each metric (accountability)
6. Data source for each metric

Format as a dashboard spec I can build in Notion, Google Sheets, or a BI tool.
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How detailed should an SOP be?

Detailed enough for a competent new hire to follow without asking you questions. The test: give the SOP to someone unfamiliar with the process and watch them do it. Every question they ask is a gap in the SOP. For complex judgment-heavy processes, use decision trees. For routine tasks, numbered steps are sufficient.

### How do I get my team to actually follow SOPs?

Make them easy to find and easy to use. Store SOPs where work happens (in your project management tool, not a separate document library nobody opens). Involve the people who do the work in writing the SOP — they'll follow procedures they helped create. Review and update SOPs quarterly — outdated SOPs breed workarounds.

### What's the ROI of documenting business processes?

The direct ROI is: (time to document) vs. (time saved per execution × number of executions × people). A 2-hour SOP that saves 15 minutes per occurrence for 3 people who do it 5 times per week pays back in 2 weeks. The indirect ROI — reduced errors, faster onboarding, ability to delegate, reduced key-person dependency — is often larger than the direct ROI.

### When should I automate vs. delegate vs. eliminate a process?

Start with eliminate: does this step need to happen at all? Then simplify: can it be done in fewer steps? Then automate: can software do this without human involvement? Then delegate: can someone less senior do this? Only after those four steps should you consider doing it yourself. Most businesses automate before eliminating or simplifying, which locks inefficiency into software.

### How many SOPs does a small business need?

Document the 20% of processes that drive 80% of your output first. For most small businesses: core product/service delivery, customer onboarding, invoicing and payment collection, hiring and onboarding, and the top 5 customer support scenarios. These 10–15 SOPs eliminate the most firefighting and enable the most delegation.
