Influencer marketing campaigns fail most often in the briefing and negotiation phase, not in the content itself. These prompts fix the work before the work — giving you professional outreach, clear briefs, and structured evaluation frameworks.

## Outreach and Pitching Prompts

### Prompt 1: Influencer Outreach Email

```
Write an influencer outreach email for:

Brand: [company name and what you sell]
Product to promote: [specific product or campaign]
Influencer profile: [their niche, platform, approximate size, why you chose them]
Campaign type: [paid post / gifted / affiliate / brand ambassador]
Compensation: [$X flat fee / commission only / gifted product / TBD]

Email requirements:
- Reference something specific about their content (leave [PERSONALIZATION] placeholder)
- Lead with what's in it for THEM (their audience fit, not your product)
- State compensation or compensation type upfront (don't make them ask)
- Give enough detail to evaluate the opportunity
- Low-pressure ask — invite a conversation, not a yes/no commitment
- Under 200 words
- Subject line: 5 options

Tone: genuine, not templated.
```

### Prompt 2: Campaign Brief Template

```
Create a complete influencer campaign brief for:

Brand: [name]
Campaign name: [name]
Campaign goal: [awareness / sales / UGC / app installs / etc.]
Product/service: [what's being promoted]
Key message: [the ONE thing creators should communicate]
Target audience: [who you're trying to reach through creators]
Content requirements: [format, length, platform, required disclosures]
Timeline: [content due date / posting window]
Compensation: [fee structure / gifting / affiliate]
Dos and Don'ts: [brand guidelines, things to avoid]

Brief sections:
1. Campaign overview (1 paragraph)
2. Brand voice guide (3 adjectives + tone examples)
3. Required content elements (mandatory mentions, links, hashtags)
4. Creative freedom (what they CAN decide themselves)
5. Approval process (how and when to submit for review)
6. Legal requirements (disclosure language, FTC compliance)
7. Metrics and reporting expectations
```

### Prompt 3: Creator Evaluation Scorecard

```
Build an influencer evaluation scorecard for selecting creators for:

Campaign type: [awareness / conversion / UGC]
Platform: [Instagram / TikTok / YouTube / LinkedIn / etc.]
Budget per creator: [$X–$Y]
Brand category: [your industry]

Create a weighted scoring system with:
1. Audience quality (fake followers check, engagement rate) — [X%] weight
2. Content quality (production, storytelling, authenticity) — [X%] weight
3. Audience alignment (demographic and psychographic match) — [X%] weight
4. Past brand work (sponsorship quality, disclosure habits) — [X%] weight
5. Reach metrics (platform-specific) — [X%] weight
6. Risk factors (controversial posts, brand safety) — [X%] weight

For each criterion: what to look for, how to measure it, red flags.
Minimum score threshold for moving to outreach: [X/100]
```

## Contract and Negotiation Prompts

### Prompt 4: Deliverables and Terms Summary

```
Draft a deliverables and key terms summary for an influencer agreement:

Creator: [their platform and size]
Campaign: [description]
Deliverables:
  - [X] [format] on [platform] by [date]
  - [X] [format] on [platform] by [date]
Usage rights: [brand can reuse content for X months on Y channels]
Exclusivity: [yes — category exclusivity for X days / no]
Revisions: [X rounds of revisions allowed]
Payment: [$X] via [payment method] within [X days of posting]
FTC disclosure: [required language]
Kill fee: [X% if brand cancels after brief approval]

Format this as a clear, plain-English deliverables summary (not a formal legal contract).
Note: all agreements should be reviewed by legal counsel before execution.
```

## Measurement Prompts

### Prompt 5: Campaign Performance Report Template

```
Create an influencer campaign performance report template for:

Campaign: [name]
Goal: [primary KPI]
Budget: [$X total]
Creators used: [X creators]
Platforms: [list]

Report sections:
1. Executive summary (campaign goal, result in one paragraph)
2. Reach and impressions by creator
3. Engagement metrics (likes, comments, saves, shares by creator)
4. Conversion metrics (link clicks, promo code uses, attributed sales)
5. Cost per [impression / click / conversion] by creator
6. Best performing content (what worked and why)
7. Worst performing content (what didn't and why)
8. Audience insights from creator analytics
9. Creator performance ranking (for future partnerships)
10. Recommendations for next campaign

Format: suitable for presenting to a marketing director or CMO.
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What's a fair rate to pay influencers in 2025?

Industry benchmarks vary significantly by platform and niche. A general starting point: Instagram/TikTok nano-influencers (1K–10K followers) $50–$300 per post; micro (10K–100K) $300–$2,000; mid-tier (100K–500K) $2,000–$10,000; macro (500K+) $10,000+. YouTube commands 2–3x Instagram rates for equivalent reach. Always factor in engagement rate over raw follower count.

### How do I find the right influencers without expensive tools?

Start on-platform: search your target keywords on Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube and note who consistently appears with genuine engagement. Check who your existing customers follow. Use free tools like Modash's free tier, Creator.co, or simply manual research. Authenticity signals (real comments, consistent niche, organic brand mentions) matter more than size.

### What's the biggest mistake brands make in influencer briefs?

Over-prescribing the creative. Briefs that dictate exact scripts, shot lists, and word choices produce inauthentic content that their audience ignores. Great briefs provide the key message, required elements, and brand guardrails — then give creators genuine creative freedom. Their audience follows them for their voice; preserve it.

### How do I handle a creator who posts content I didn't approve?

Address it immediately and professionally. Reference the contract terms around approval process. If the content violates brand guidelines significantly, request it be taken down (your contract should specify this right). If it's borderline, evaluate whether the audience reaction justifies leaving it up. Prevention is better: a clear approval workflow in the brief (Prompt 2) prevents most situations.

### Is influencer marketing worth it for small budgets?

Micro and nano influencers ($100–$500 per post) consistently outperform larger creators on engagement rate and conversion, especially for niche products. A $2,000 budget spread across 10 highly relevant micro-influencers often outperforms a single $2,000 post from a mid-tier creator with a broad, less aligned audience.
