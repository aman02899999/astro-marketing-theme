Getting press coverage changes everything — search rankings improve, credibility compounds, and sales conversations get shorter. But most pitches fail in the subject line. These prompts help you write press materials that journalists actually read.

## Press Release Prompts

### Prompt 1: Press Release Writer

```
Write a press release for:

News: [what's happening — product launch / funding / partnership / milestone / research]
Company: [name and one-sentence description]
Announcement date: [date]
Key facts:
  - [Fact 1: the news in one sentence]
  - [Fact 2: supporting context]
  - [Fact 3: why this matters]
Quote from: [name, title]
Quote theme: [what the quote should convey — excitement / impact / vision]
Boilerplate: [2-sentence standard company description]

Press release format:
FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE (or embargoed date)
Headline: [news-focused, under 100 characters]
Subheadline: [one additional context sentence]
Dateline: [City, Date] —
Lead paragraph: who, what, when, where, why (5Ws in under 50 words)
Body: 2–3 paragraphs with supporting details and context
Quote: [from an executive]
Boilerplate: ###

AP style. No jargon. Write for a journalist who knows nothing about your company.
```

### Prompt 2: Media Pitch Email

```
Write a journalist pitch email for:

Story angle: [what story you're pitching — not your press release, a genuine story idea]
Target publication: [outlet name and type]
Target journalist: [their beat/coverage area]
News hook: [why this story is relevant right now]
What I can offer: [exclusive / data / expert source / unique access]
My company/client: [brief description — keep background minimal]

Email requirements:
- Subject line: news hook, not your company name (5 options)
- First sentence: the story, not the pitch
- No "I hope this email finds you well"
- Under 150 words for the body
- One clear ask: "Would you like to cover this?" or "Would you be interested in speaking with [expert]?"
- Follow-up plan: one follow-up, one week later, then let it go

Rule: pitch a story, not a company. Journalists serve their readers, not your PR goals.
```

### Prompt 3: Media Interview Prep

```
Prepare me for a media interview:

Publication/show: [name and format — podcast / TV / print / online]
Journalist: [their focus area]
Topic: [what you'll be interviewed about]
My position/expertise: [who you are and why you're being interviewed]
Key messages I want to get across: [list 2–3 core points]
Questions I expect: [list likely questions]
Questions I'm nervous about: [the hard ones]

Prepare:
1. My elevator pitch (30 seconds — who I am and why I'm the right person to talk to)
2. Key message 1, 2, 3 — each with a bridge phrase to get back to it
3. Answers to expected questions (concise — under 45 seconds each)
4. Responses to the difficult questions (honest, not evasive)
5. Sound-bite phrases (memorable, quotable sentences)
6. What NOT to say (phrases that could be taken out of context)
7. How to end the interview (what I want the last thing they hear to be)
```

## Crisis Communication Prompts

### Prompt 4: Crisis Statement

```
Write a crisis communication statement for:

Situation: [describe the crisis — product failure / data breach / public controversy / negative press]
Audience: [customers / investors / employees / general public — or all]
Stage: [first 24 hours / 48–72 hours / ongoing]
What happened: [factual description — no spin]
What we're doing about it: [concrete actions taken or planned]
What we don't yet know: [unanswered questions]

Statement requirements:
- Acknowledge the situation directly (no minimizing)
- Express genuine concern for those affected
- State what we know, what we're doing, and what we don't know yet
- Avoid: "We take this very seriously" (used, meaningless), legal non-admissions that sound evasive
- Give a timeline for when we'll have more information
- One clear spokesperson and contact

Length: under 200 words. This will be read by journalists under pressure.
```

## Thought Leadership Prompts

### Prompt 5: Expert Commentary Pitch

```
Write an expert commentary pitch:

Expert: [name and credentials]
Topic: [current news event or trend they can comment on]
Their unique angle: [what perspective do they bring that others don't?]
Target: [specific journalist, outlet, or "HARO-style" opportunity]
Timing: [this is time-sensitive — hook to current news]

Pitch format:
- Subject: "[Breaking/Trending topic]: Expert available to comment"
- Opening: tie to the current news hook
- Expert credential line (one sentence — most relevant credential only)
- 2–3 sentences of their unique take (the actual insight, not "happy to discuss")
- Availability and contact
- Under 100 words total

Also write: 3 ready-to-use quotes from the expert on this topic (for quick placement).
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What makes a press release newsworthy?

Five tests: Is it new? (recency) Is it relevant to the publication's readers? (relevance) Does it affect or interest many people? (significance) Is it unexpected or counterintuitive? (novelty) Is there a human element? (human interest). If your news doesn't pass at least 3 of these tests, it's a marketing announcement, not press-worthy. Reframe or hold the release.

### How do I find the right journalist to pitch?

Read the publication you want to be in, find who covers your topic, and read their last 10 articles. Pitch only when your story genuinely fits their beat. Unsolicited bulk pitching to irrelevant journalists destroys your future pitching reputation. One targeted pitch to the right journalist beats 50 generic blasts.

### How quickly should I respond to a media crisis?

Within 2 hours for initial acknowledgment, even if just: "We're aware of the situation and are investigating. We'll have a full statement by [specific time]." Silence in a crisis is interpreted as guilt. Use Prompt 4's statement framework to move faster — the hardest part is deciding what to say, not the writing.

### Is a press release still relevant in 2025?

For journalists: less so than a decade ago — they prefer personalized pitches. For SEO and wire distribution (PR Newswire, Business Wire, Globe Newswire): yes — press releases on wire services earn backlinks and can rank in Google News. For internal announcement distribution, investor relations, and documentation: absolutely. Use the right format for the right goal.

### How do I build media relationships without a PR budget?

Engage authentically before you need coverage. Follow target journalists on social media, share their work, respond thoughtfully to their posts. Become a helpful source — when they write about your industry, offer data, quotes, or expert connections even when you're not the story. Journalists remember sources who helped them without asking for anything in return.
