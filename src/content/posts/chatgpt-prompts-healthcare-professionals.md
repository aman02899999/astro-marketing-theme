> **Medical disclaimer:** These prompts assist with documentation and patient communication. All clinical content must be reviewed by qualified healthcare professionals before use. Never use AI-generated content as a substitute for clinical judgment.

Healthcare professionals spend up to 35% of their time on documentation. These prompts reduce that burden while improving the quality of patient-facing materials and clinical communication.

## Patient Education Prompts

### Prompt 1: Patient Education Handout

```
Write a patient education handout for:

Condition/procedure/medication: [name]
Target patient: [age, health literacy level — low/medium/high, language preference]
Reading level: [grade 6–8 for general population]
Key messages patient must understand:
1. [what this is]
2. [what they need to do]
3. [warning signs to watch for]
4. [when to seek help]

Format:
- Plain language (no jargon — define any medical terms)
- Short paragraphs (3–4 sentences maximum)
- Bullet points for instructions and warning signs
- Bold key terms
- Clear "Call your doctor if..." section
- Next appointment reminder placeholder

Avoid: passive voice, complex sentences, assuming health literacy.
```

---

### Prompt 2: Discharge Instructions

```
Write discharge instructions for a patient with: [condition/procedure]

Patient situation: [brief clinical context]
Target understanding: [what the patient must know to safely recover at home]
Medications to manage: [list or describe]
Activity restrictions: [describe]
Follow-up: [timeframe and who to see]
Warning signs: [symptoms requiring immediate return]

Instructions must include:
1. What happened / what was done (plain language)
2. What to expect in recovery (timeline)
3. Activity and dietary guidance
4. Medication instructions (when, how, what to watch for)
5. Wound care or equipment instructions (if applicable)
6. Emergency warning signs (when to call 911 vs. call the office)
7. Follow-up appointment reminder
8. Important phone numbers

Format: clear enough that a stressed, just-discharged patient can follow them.
```

## Clinical Documentation Prompts

### Prompt 3: Patient Communication Letter

```
Draft a patient communication letter for:

Purpose: [test result notification / referral / treatment plan / follow-up recommendation]
Clinical information to communicate: [describe what the patient needs to know]
Tone: [reassuring / informative / urgent]
Patient: [general adult / elderly / pediatric parent]
Action required from patient: [what they need to do]

Letter requirements:
- Opening: state the purpose in the first sentence
- Clinical information: plain language, not jargon
- What it means for the patient (not just the finding)
- Clear next steps with specifics (who to call, when, where)
- Contact information for questions
- Professional but warm tone

I will review and adjust all clinical details before sending.
```

### Prompt 4: Health Literacy Simplifier

```
Simplify this clinical text for a patient with low health literacy:

Original text: [paste clinical language — discharge summary / lab result explanation / treatment description]

Target: Grade 6 reading level
Audience: adult patient, possibly anxious, reading this at home

Simplify by:
1. Replace medical terms with plain equivalents
2. Break long sentences into short ones
3. Use active voice throughout
4. Replace passive constructions ("it was found that") with direct statements
5. Add brief explanations for any term that must be kept
6. Keep the same clinical meaning — don't omit important information

Show original alongside simplified version for clinical review.
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can ChatGPT write clinical notes or medical records?

ChatGPT can draft templates and assist with structure, but clinical documentation must be completed and signed by the licensed provider who assessed the patient. AI-generated clinical notes used without proper review and attestation raise serious liability and compliance issues. Use these prompts for patient-facing materials and communication templates, not for official medical records.

### How do I ensure patient education materials are medically accurate?

Generate the draft with ChatGPT, then have a qualified clinician (physician, NP, pharmacist) review for medical accuracy before distribution. ChatGPT's medical knowledge reflects its training data and may not reflect the most current clinical guidelines. The regulatory and clinical review step is non-optional for patient-facing health materials.

### What health literacy level should patient education materials target?

The American Medical Association recommends 6th to 8th grade reading level for patient education materials intended for general adult populations. Use Prompt 1's reading level specification and verify with a readability tool (Hemingway App, Readable.com) after generating. Research consistently shows even highly educated patients prefer plain-language health materials.

### How do I use these prompts to reduce documentation burden?

Start with discharge instructions (Prompt 2) — these are time-consuming and follow predictable structures that ChatGPT handles well. Use the output as a starting template you customize per patient in 5 minutes rather than writing from scratch in 20. Over a year, this saves hundreds of hours for busy clinicians.

### Are there HIPAA concerns with using ChatGPT for patient materials?

Use only de-identified information when interacting with ChatGPT or any AI tool not covered by a Business Associate Agreement (BAA). OpenAI offers BAA-covered enterprise plans for healthcare settings. Do not paste specific patient information (name, DOB, MRN, specific clinical details that could identify a patient) into public AI tools without appropriate data governance in place.
