Copywriting formulas exist because they work. They're not templates for generic writing — they're structures for persuasion that have been tested across millions of words and billions of dollars in ad spend. These prompts apply the 10 most powerful formulas to your specific projects.

## The Core Formulas

### Prompt 1: AIDA Framework

```
Write copy using the AIDA formula for:

Product/service: [description]
Audience: [who this is for]
Goal: [what action you want]
Channel: [landing page / email / ad / social]

ATTENTION: [hook that stops the right person cold]
INTEREST: [build intrigue with a compelling problem or insight]
DESIRE: [show the transformation — benefits, proof, specificity]
ACTION: [clear, low-friction CTA]

Write 3 variations using different attention hooks:
1. Problem-aware hook
2. Solution-aware hook
3. Curiosity/intrigue hook

Length: [short for ads / medium for email / long for landing page]
```

### Prompt 2: PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solution)

```
Write copy using the PAS formula for:

Product/service: [description]
Core customer pain: [the real frustration — be specific]
Stakes: [what happens if the problem isn't solved]
Solution: [how you fix it]
Audience: [detailed description]

PROBLEM: State the problem clearly and specifically — name the pain the reader recognizes immediately

AGITATE: Make the problem feel more urgent and real
- The emotional cost
- The time cost
- What they've already tried that didn't work
- What they stand to lose if nothing changes

SOLUTION: Your product/service as the relief
- How it solves the problem
- What changes for them
- Proof that it works
- The ask

Write for [email body / landing page / sales call opening].
```

### Prompt 3: Before-After-Bridge

```
Write Before-After-Bridge copy for:

Product: [description]
Target customer: [description]
Channel: [email / landing page / social]

BEFORE: Describe the reader's world right now
- The frustration they live with
- What they've tried
- How it makes them feel

AFTER: Describe their world with your solution
- What's different
- What's possible now
- How their day/business/life changes

BRIDGE: Your product as the path from Before to After
- What it is
- How it works
- Why it's the right bridge for them
- The ask
```

### Prompt 4: The 4 Ps (Promise-Picture-Proof-Push)

```
Write copy using the 4 Ps for:

Offer: [product / service / opt-in / event]
Target: [audience]
Key outcome: [transformation promised]
Proof available: [testimonials / data / case studies / before-after]

PROMISE: Lead with the transformation (not the product)
- One compelling headline-level promise

PICTURE: Paint the future state vividly
- What their life/business looks like after
- Sensory, specific, emotional

PROOF: Back up the promise with evidence
- Social proof (testimonials, numbers, logos)
- Technical proof (how it works)
- Risk reversal (guarantee)

PUSH: The final nudge to act
- Reinforce urgency or scarcity (if genuine)
- Clear CTA
- Remove final objection
```

### Prompt 5: StoryBrand Framework

```
Apply the StoryBrand framework to my [brand / product / landing page]:

Company: [name and description]
Product/service: [what you offer]
Target customer: [the hero of the story]

Map the 7 elements:
1. CHARACTER: [the customer — who they are and what they want]
2. HAS A PROBLEM: [external problem + internal feeling + philosophical problem]
3. AND MEETS A GUIDE: [my company — with empathy + authority]
4. WHO GIVES THEM A PLAN: [the 3-step process I offer]
5. AND CALLS THEM TO ACTION: [direct CTA / transitional CTA]
6. THAT HELPS THEM AVOID FAILURE: [what they lose if they don't act]
7. AND ENDS IN SUCCESS: [the transformation/happy ending]

Output: website homepage copy using this framework.
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which copywriting formula works best for email?

PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solution) consistently produces the highest response rates in email because it starts where the reader already is — in their problem — rather than asking them to care about your solution immediately. AIDA works well for announcement emails. Before-After-Bridge works for launch sequences.

### Should I always follow a formula exactly?

No — use formulas as scaffolding, not cages. The best copywriters internalize the principles behind formulas (clarity, specificity, emotional resonance, clear CTA) and adapt them to the context. A formula used mechanically produces mechanical copy. A formula used as a thinking tool produces compelling, persuasive writing.

### How do I know which formula to use?

Match formula to awareness level. PAS for audiences who know they have the problem but haven't heard of you. AIDA for audiences who are problem-aware and solution-aware. StoryBrand for brand positioning where the customer needs to see themselves as the hero. Before-After-Bridge for any context where transformation is the selling proposition.

### What's the difference between features and benefits in copywriting?

Features are what a product has or does. Benefits are what the customer gets or feels. "256GB storage" (feature) vs. "thousands of songs, photos, and files at your fingertips — never delete anything again" (benefit). The rule: always translate features into benefits for consumer copy. For technical audiences, features matter alongside benefits.

### How do I write copy that doesn't sound like AI?

Specific beats generic every time. "You'll save time" is AI language. "You'll get that hour back on Monday morning" is human language. Add specificity, personality, and concrete examples at every opportunity. Read it aloud — if it sounds like something nobody would say in conversation, rewrite it. ChatGPT-generated copy improved with specific details and voice sounds human.
