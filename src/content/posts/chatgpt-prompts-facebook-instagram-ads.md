Facebook and Instagram ads live or die in the first three words. If the hook doesn't stop the scroll, nothing else matters. These prompts help you write ad copy that stops, engages, and converts — for any product, offer, or audience.

## Ad Copy Fundamentals Prompts

### Prompt 1: Full Ad Copy Set

```
Write a complete Facebook/Instagram ad copy set for:

Product/service: [description]
Target audience: [demographics + psychographics + pain points]
Offer: [what they get — free trial / discount / lead magnet / product]
Landing page CTA: [what happens when they click]
Objective: [awareness / traffic / conversions / leads]
Budget level: [testing budget under $50/day / scaling over $100/day]

For each ad produce:
Primary text: [main copy — 125 characters for mobile view, full version]
Headline: [27 characters — the most important line]
Description: [27 characters — supporting the headline]
CTA button: [Shop Now / Learn More / Sign Up / Get Quote]

Write 3 complete variations:
Variation A: Pain-point focused
Variation B: Outcome/aspiration focused
Variation C: Social proof / trust focused
```

### Prompt 2: Hook Line Generator

```
Write 20 ad hook lines for:

Product: [product/service]
Target customer pain: [specific frustration they have]
Target customer desire: [what they want instead]

Hook types (4 each):
1. "If you [situation], then [product] is for you..."
2. Question hook: "[Question that makes them say 'that's me']"
3. Statistic hook: "[Surprising number] [people / businesses] [problem]"
4. Contrast hook: "Most [audience] [wrong behavior]. Here's what actually works."
5. Benefit hook: "Finally, [outcome] without [common obstacle]"

For each: rate the emotional intensity (1–10) and the specificity (1–10).
Flag the top 5 to test first.
```

## Audience and Targeting Prompts

### Prompt 3: Audience Persona for Ad Targeting

```
Create a detailed ad targeting persona for:

Product: [description]
My current best customers: [describe 2–3 real customers if possible]

For Meta/Facebook targeting, define:
1. Age range
2. Gender (if relevant)
3. Location targeting approach
4. Interest categories (list 15–20 specific interest keywords to test)
5. Behavioral targeting signals
6. Life events relevant to this product
7. Lookalike audience seed (who to base lookalikes on)
8. Audiences to EXCLUDE (who burns budget without converting)

Also describe the psychology of this customer:
- What they tell themselves about their problem
- What they've already tried
- What objection will stop them from clicking
- What would make them buy immediately
```

## Retargeting Prompts

### Prompt 4: Retargeting Ad Copy

```
Write retargeting ad copy for people who:

Situation: [visited product page / added to cart / started checkout / watched 50% of video / etc.]
Product: [description]
Why they might not have converted: [common objections or friction]
Offer or incentive: [discount / free shipping / urgency / social proof / none]

Write 3 retargeting ads:

Ad 1: Objection handler (address the specific reason they likely left)
Ad 2: Social proof angle (reviews, numbers, trust signals)
Ad 3: Urgency or scarcity (if genuine — don't fabricate)

Each ad: primary text + headline + CTA.
Keep tone warm, not desperate.
```

## Testing and Optimization Prompts

### Prompt 5: A/B Test Planning

```
Design an A/B test plan for Facebook ads:

Campaign goal: [objective]
Current best-performing ad: [describe it]
Budget available for testing: [$X per day]
Test duration: [X days]

Design 5 meaningful A/B tests to run sequentially (not simultaneously):

Test 1: [element to test, e.g. hook line]
Test 2: [element to test, e.g. visual type]
Test 3: [element to test, e.g. offer framing]
Test 4: [element to test, e.g. CTA button]
Test 5: [element to test, e.g. audience]

For each test:
- Hypothesis (what I expect to happen and why)
- Control version
- Variant version
- Winning metric to measure
- Minimum results needed to call a winner
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long should Facebook ad primary text be?

Test both. Short (under 125 characters — visible without "see more") for broad cold audiences. Long (200–500 words) for warm retargeting audiences who need more persuasion. Most advertisers only test short copy. Long copy can dramatically outperform for high-ticket offers.

### What's the most important element of a Facebook ad?

The creative (image or video) gets the first impression, but the hook line in the primary text or video determines whether they stop. Spend 50% of your creative testing budget on different hooks. A great hook with a mediocre image will beat a great image with a weak hook.

### How many ad variations should I test at once?

For new campaigns: 3–5 variations maximum. Running too many splits your budget too thin and takes longer to reach statistical significance. Test one variable at a time — either the hook, the image, the offer, or the audience. Never change two things at once or you won't know what drove the difference.

### Why is my CTR high but conversion rate low?

The ad is attracting clicks from people who aren't your buyer (wrong audience or misleading hook) or the landing page doesn't match the ad's promise. Audit your audience targeting and compare the ad's implied promise against what the landing page delivers in the first 5 seconds.

### What's a good ROAS to aim for?

It depends entirely on your margins. A 3x ROAS on a 30% margin product is unprofitable. A 2x ROAS on an 80% margin SaaS product is excellent. Calculate your break-even ROAS first: if your COGS is 40% of revenue, break-even ROAS is 1/(1-0.40) = 1.67x. Target at least 2x break-even as your minimum viable ROAS.
